# 1. Overview & principles

Evidence tags: **[MA]** meta-analysis · **[RCT]** controlled trial(s) · **[Mech]** biomechanical reasoning only.

- **Volume.** Muscle growth rises with weekly sets, with diminishing returns [MA: Schoenfeld 2017; Pelland et al.]. Pelland's meta-regression found that counting indirect work as **half a set** fit the data best, so I use that rule. In trained lifters, 12, 18 and 24 weekly quad sets gave similar thickness gains (Aubé 2022). So the plan targets about **10–17 effective sets per muscle** and treats extra sets as a targeted tool, not the default.
- **Frequency.** When volume is equal, frequency adds little to growth on its own [MA: Schoenfeld 2019; Pelland]. Training each muscle twice a week is mainly a way to cap a session at about 6–10 sets per muscle, so set quality stays high.
- **Load.** Growth is similar across roughly 5–30 reps when sets end close to failure [MA: Schoenfeld 2017]. So compounds use 5–10 reps (efficient and easy to progress) and isolations use 8–20 (easier on joints and safe to push hard).
- **Proximity to failure.** Failure isn't required [MA: Refalo 2023], but growth seems to improve as sets end closer to it [MA: Robinson 2024, exploratory]. In trained lifters, failure and 1–2 RIR gave the same quad growth, but failure caused more fatigue [RCT: Refalo 2024]. RIR (reps in reserve) is how many more good reps you could have done.
  - Compounds stop at **1–2 RIR**, and squats and RDLs at 2 because you train alone.
  - Isolations go to **0–1 RIR**, and only their last set goes to true failure.
  - People misjudge RIR by 1–2 reps, more so further from failure (Halperin 2022), so week 1 includes a calibration step.
- **Long muscle lengths.** Training a muscle where it is stretched produced more growth in several trials [RCT]:
  - Hamstrings: seated leg curls beat lying leg curls (Maeo 2021).
  - Triceps: overhead extensions gave about 1.4× the growth of arms-down extensions (Maeo 2023).
  - Biceps: the stretched half of the curl grew the lower biceps more (Pedrosa 2023).
  - Calves: stretched-range partial reps beat top-range partials (Kassiano 2023).

  **The catch:** in trained lifters, stretched-range partial reps ("lengthened partials") matched full range of motion but didn't beat it (Wolf 2025). **My rule:** use full range on exercises that load the muscle hard at its longest point, and add lengthened partials only to extend the last set after you fail at full range.
- **Matching the resistance curve.** The term you want is the external *resistance profile* (load × lever arm across the range) set against the muscle's *strength curve*. The force-velocity relationship isn't something you match through exercise choice. It matters because the slow final reps before failure put the most tension on muscle fibers. I paired exercises that load hardest in the stretched position (DB flyes, preacher curls, RDLs, low-cable laterals) with ones that load the mid-range (presses, rows).
- **Rest.** 3-minute rests beat 1-minute rests [RCT: Schoenfeld 2016]. A 2024 Bayesian meta-analysis found a small benefit from resting more than 60 s and no clear extra benefit past about 90 s [MA: Singer 2024]. The likely reason is that longer rest preserves reps and load, not "complete neurological recovery." **The real rule: rest until you can hit your target reps at your target RIR.**
- **Tempo.** Rep durations from 0.5 to 8 seconds give similar growth [MA: Schoenfeld 2015]. Lower the weight under control over 2–3 s, pause briefly in the stretch on isolations, and lift with intent.
- **EMG doesn't predict growth.** Muscle activation measured with EMG doesn't reliably predict hypertrophy (Vigotsky 2022). In Plotkin 2023, hip thrusts showed higher glute EMG but didn't grow the glutes more than squats. No exercise here was chosen on EMG alone.

# 2. Weekly microcycle

| Day | Session | Focus | Sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Push A** | Heavy horizontal press, chest | 17 |
| Tue | **Pull A** | Vertical pull, lats | 16 |
| Wed | **Legs A** | Squat, quads | 14 |
| Thu | **Push B** | Incline and overhead, delts | 18 |
| Fri | **Pull B** | One-arm work, upper back | 17 |
| Sat | **Legs B** | Hip hinge, hamstrings | 19 |
| Sun | Rest | | |

**Design logic:**
- **A and B days use different exercises.** That gives you planned variation, which supports more even growth across a muscle (Fonseca 2014; Kassiano 2022 review). It also means each lift is trained once a week, so you track progress week to week.
- **Spinal loading is spread out.** Squats (Wed) and RDLs (Sat) never fall on back-to-back days.
- **Pull days spare the lower back.** Both use chest-supported or braced rows, so your posterior chain is fresh the next day for squats or RDLs.

**Weekly volume** (effective sets count indirect work as 0.5 per set):

| Muscle | Direct sets | Effective sets |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | 14 | ~14 |
| Front delts | 3 | ~10 (pressing covers them) |
| Side delts | 10 | ~11.5 |
| Rear delts | 6 | ~15 |
| Lats | 13 | ~16 |
| Mid/upper traps, rhomboids | 9 | ~17 |
| Biceps | 8 | ~17 |
| Triceps | 8 | ~14 |
| Quads | 13 | 13 |
| Hamstrings | 10 | 10 |
| Glutes | 7 (squat, split squat) | ~10–11 |
| Calves | 10 | 10 |

**Warm-up:** 5 min of easy cardio. Before the first compound, do about 3 ramp-up sets (roughly 50% × 8, 70% × 4, 85% × 2). Before later exercises, do one lighter feeder set for any muscle you haven't trained yet that session.

# 3. Full exercise breakdowns

## PUSH A (Monday)

**A1. Barbell Bench Press — 3 × 5–8 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 3–4 min**
- **Targets:** sternal (mid) pec ▸ front delts, triceps
- **Why:** It's the heaviest horizontal press you can load, so it's the easiest place to keep adding weight for the pecs. Load torque and the pec's leverage are both highest near the chest, where the pec is stretched, so the bottom of a full-range rep is already a loaded stretch. It goes first because exercise order affects performance.
- **Evidence:** Flat and incline pressing grew the middle and lower pec similarly, and incline favored the upper pec (Chaves 2020, untrained lifters training once a week, so moderate confidence). Heavy sets of 5–8 grow muscle as well as moderate reps when taken close to failure (Schoenfeld 2017 load MA).
- **Cues:** Set the safety bars 1–2 cm below your chest, because you train alone. Grip about 1.5× shoulder width. Pull your shoulder blades back and down and use a moderate arch. Touch the lower sternum with forearms vertical and elbows 45–70° from your torso. Touch briefly; don't bounce.

**A2. Incline DB Press (30°) — 3 × 8–12 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 2.5–3 min**
- **Targets:** clavicular (upper) pec ▸ front delts, triceps
- **Why:** Upper-pec fibers run up toward the upper arm, so the incline lines up with them. Dumbbells let your elbows drop below your torso for a deeper stretch and let your hands come together at the top.
- **Evidence:** Chaves 2020 found incline beat flat for upper-pec thickness. EMG work puts peak upper-pec activity around 30°, with front-delt involvement rising at steeper angles (Rodríguez-Ridao 2020). That EMG result is supporting evidence only.
- **Cues:** Set the bench to 30°, not 45°. Lower until your upper arms are slightly below your torso and you feel a strong chest stretch. Press up and slightly in, and kick the DBs up off your knees to start.

**A3. Deep-Stretch DB Flye — 2 × 10–15 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 2 min**
- **Targets:** sternal pec ▸ front delts
- **Why:** Its resistance profile is the opposite of the bench press. Torque peaks with your arms wide, where the pec is longest, and is near zero at the top. That makes it a stretched-position complement to pressing that costs your triceps and elbows very little.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics plus the long-length trials above (Maeo, Pedrosa, Kassiano). Because of Wolf 2025, use full range, with partials only after failure.
- **Cues:** Fix a 15–20° bend in your elbows and keep your shoulder blades pulled back. Lower in an arc until your upper arms sit just below the bench and your chest is deeply stretched, but your shoulder doesn't hurt. Stop about 30° short of vertical at the top, since there's no tension there. On the last set, add 4–6 bottom-half partials after you fail at full range.

**A4. Single-Arm Low-Pulley Cable Lateral Raise — 5 × 10–15 @ 0–1 RIR · alternate arms, 60–90 s between sides**
- **Targets:** side delts ▸ supraspinatus, upper traps
- **Why:** With a dumbbell, torque is zero at the bottom and highest at the top, where the side delt is shortest. A cable from the low pulley, crossing your body, loads the bottom where the delt is longest and keeps tension fairly even. Side delts get almost nothing from pressing, so they get the most direct sets of any delt.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics for the exercise choice. Pelland's fractional counting for the volume.
- **Cues:** Stand side-on to the low pulley with the handle in your far hand and lean slightly away. Raise slightly in front of your body (about 20°) to shoulder height, leading with the elbow. Start each rep from across your midline and take 2–3 s on the way down. If the machine's footplate gets in the way, use the lean-away DB raise from Push B.

**A5. Overhead Cable Triceps Extension (rope, high pulley) — 4 × 10–15 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 90 s–2 min**
- **Targets:** triceps long head ▸ medial and lateral heads
- **Why:** The long head crosses the shoulder, so raising your arm overhead stretches it. The cable keeps tension at the deep stretch, where a dumbbell's torque fades.
- **Evidence:** In Maeo 2023 (EJSS, 12 weeks, MRI), overhead cable extensions grew the triceps about 1.4× more than arms-down extensions. The long head grew 28.5% vs 19.6%, even though the overhead arm lifted lighter loads.
- **Cues:** Face away from the pulldown in a staggered stance, leaning forward 30–45° with your upper arms beside your ears. Let your hands sink deep behind your head, then extend fully without your elbows flaring or drifting down.

## PULL A (Tuesday)

**C1. Lat Pulldown — 4 × 6–10 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 2.5–3 min**
- **Targets:** lats, teres major ▸ biceps, rear delts, lower traps
- **Why:** Vertical pulling works the lats from full overhead reach, where they're longest. The weight stack loads that stretch evenly through the range. The thigh pad means your bodyweight doesn't cap the load, which it would on pull-ups.
- **Evidence:** In trained lifters, full range matched lengthened partials, and pulldowns were one of the tested exercises (Wolf 2025). So use full range with a real stretch. Grip-width differences are small and mostly EMG-based, so pick the grip that gives you the deepest pain-free stretch.
- **Cues:** Grip 1.25–1.5× shoulder width, or use a neutral grip. At the top, let your shoulder blades rise fully for 1 s. Start each rep by pulling your shoulder blades down, then drive your elbows toward your hips with about a 15° lean back. Bring the bar to your upper chest and take 2–3 s on the way up. **Swap:** weighted pull-ups if your rack has a bar.

**C2. Chest-Supported Incline DB Row — 3 × 8–12 @ 1 RIR · rest 2–2.5 min**
- **Targets:** mid/lower traps, rhomboids, lats ▸ rear delts, biceps
- **Why:** The bench takes your lower back and hamstrings out of the lift, so your upper back is what limits you. That gives more growth stimulus for the fatigue, and it keeps your posterior chain fresh for tomorrow's squats. Dumbbells also let your shoulder blades spread fully at the bottom, the stretched position.
- **Evidence:** Fatigue logic: Refalo 2024 found extra fatigue that didn't buy extra growth. Plus biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Set the bench to 30–45° and keep your chest on the pad. Let your shoulders round forward fully at the bottom, then pull your elbows back about 45° from your torso. Squeeze for 1 s at the top and don't shrug.

**C3. Seated Cable Row (close/neutral grip) — 3 × 10–12 @ 1 RIR · rest 2 min**
- **Targets:** lats ▸ mid-back, rear delts, biceps
- **Why:** Keeping your elbows tucked and pulling toward your hips makes this lat-dominant. The cable keeps load on at full reach, with your arm forward and shoulder blades spread, a lat stretch a dumbbell row can't load as well.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics, plus Wolf 2025 on using full range.
- **Cues:** Use two D-handles, or the straight bar with a narrow grip. At the stretch, reach forward and lean 10–15°. Then sit tall and drive your elbows toward your back pockets. Keep torso movement to 15° or less.

**C4. Face Pull (rope, high pulley) — 3 × 12–15 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 90 s**
- **Targets:** rear delts ▸ mid/lower traps, rotator cuff (infraspinatus, teres minor)
- **Why:** It combines pulling your arms apart with rotating them outward, and the cable loads the arms-forward stretched position. It also balances out all the pressing for shoulder health.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Sit on the floor or kneel so the rope comes from slightly above you. Pull to your forehead with elbows high and wide, and finish with your fists beside your ears and thumbs pointing back. Hold for 1 s.

**C5. Incline DB Curl (bench 45–60°) — 3 × 8–12 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 90 s–2 min**
- **Targets:** biceps (long head emphasis) ▸ brachialis
- **Why:** The biceps crosses the shoulder, so letting your arm hang behind your body stretches it, especially the long head. Every rep happens at a longer muscle length than a standing curl.
- **Evidence:** Training the stretched half of the curl produced more growth in the lower biceps (Pedrosa 2023; 19 untrained women, so moderate confidence).
- **Cues:** Let your arms hang straight down behind your torso and keep your elbows back as you curl. Turn your palms up hard and take 2–3 s back to full extension. On the last set, add lengthened partials.

## LEGS A (Wednesday)

**E1. High-Bar Back Squat (full depth) — 4 × 5–8 @ 2 RIR (1 RIR in weeks 4–5) · rest 3–4 min**
- **Targets:** quads ▸ glutes, adductors, lower back
- **Why:** It's the heaviest knee-dominant lift you can load, and full depth loads the glutes and adductors at long lengths. Stay at 2 RIR because you train alone and failing a barbell squat costs a lot of extra fatigue.
- **Evidence:**
  - Full squats grew the glutes and adductors more than half squats, with similar quad growth (Kubo 2019, MRI).
  - Squats grew the glutes about as much as hip thrusts, plus more quads and adductors (Plotkin 2023).
  - Training to failure gave the same growth as 1–2 RIR, with more fatigue (Refalo 2024).
- **Cues:** Set the safeties just below your bottom position. Brace hard all around your torso and take 2 s to descend below parallel, knees tracking over your toes. Drive up while keeping the same chest angle.

**E2. DB Bulgarian Split Squat — 3 × 8–12 per leg @ 1–2 RIR · 60 s between legs, 2 min between rounds**
- **Targets:** quads, glutes ▸ adductors
- **Why:** The front leg goes into deep hip and knee bend with little load on your spine, so it adds a lot of stimulus for little fatigue after squats. You can shift the emphasis: a longer stance with a forward lean favors glutes, a shorter stance with an upright torso favors quads.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics, plus Kubo 2019 on depth and long-length glute and adductor work.
- **Cues:** Rear foot laces-down on the bench. Descend until your rear knee is about an inch from the floor and keep your front heel down. Use straps if grip limits you.

**E3. Nordic Hamstring Curl — 3 × 4–8 lowering reps · rest 2–3 min**
- **Targets:** hamstrings (semitendinosus, short head of the biceps femoris) ▸ calves (gastrocnemius)
- **Why:** You don't have a leg-curl machine, and the Nordic is the strongest knee-flexion option. The lowering effort peaks as your knees straighten, where the hamstrings are longest. RIR doesn't apply here: end each set when you can no longer control the descent through the full range.
- **Evidence:** Nordics grew the semitendinosus as much as hip-extension training did, but hip extension grew the long head of the biceps femoris more (Bourne 2017). That's why Legs B has RDLs and a hip-flexed leg curl. Low Nordic volume lengthened the biceps femoris long head's muscle fibers as well as high volume did (Presland 2018).
- **Cues:**
  - Anchor your heels under a padded barbell racked low, or under the pulldown thigh pad.
  - Keep a straight line from knees to shoulders without bending at the hips, and take 3–5 s to lower.
  - Catch yourself with your hands and push back lightly.
  - **In week 1, do only 2 × 3–5**, because first-time soreness is severe.
  - Loop a band around the rack for assistance if needed.

**E4. Standing Single-Leg DB Calf Raise — 4 × 8–12 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 60–90 s**
- **Targets:** gastrocnemius ▸ soleus
- **Why:** A straight knee keeps the gastrocnemius long, since it crosses the knee. Doing it on one leg on a step gives a deep stretch and enough load with dumbbells.
- **Evidence:** In Kassiano 2023, bottom-range partials grew the medial gastrocnemius about 15%, vs about 7% for full range and 3% for top-range partials. The bottom-range vs full-range difference wasn't statistically significant, and the subjects were untrained women.
- **Cues:** Put the ball of your foot on a plate stack or step and hold the rack. Pause 2 s at the bottom so your Achilles tendon can't bounce you up, then rise fully. On the last set, add bottom-half partials.

## PUSH B (Thursday)

**B1. Incline Barbell Press (30°) — 3 × 6–10 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 3 min**
- **Targets:** upper pec ▸ front delts, triceps
- **Why:** It keeps the upper-chest priority in a heavier form than Monday's dumbbells. The different tool and rep range is planned variation, not random variation.
- **Evidence:** Chaves 2020. The Kassiano 2022 review found planned variation can improve regional growth, while excessive variation can hurt it.
- **Cues:** Set the safeties and the bench to 30°. Touch your high chest, just below the collarbones, with wrists stacked over elbows. Set your shoulder blades the same way as on the bench press.

**B2. Seated DB Shoulder Press (back support ~80°) — 3 × 8–12 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 2–3 min**
- **Targets:** front delts ▸ side delts, triceps, upper pec
- **Why:** Front delts already get about 7 effective sets from pressing, so one vertical press is enough. Dumbbells allow a deeper bottom position, and the back pad removes lower-back strain, which beats a standing barbell press on stimulus for the fatigue.
- **Evidence:** Volume logic from Pelland's fractional counting.
- **Cues:** Keep your elbows slightly in front of your torso and lower to ear or chin level. Press slightly inward and stop just short of lockout. Keep your back on the pad.

**B3. Low-to-High Single-Arm Cable Fly (low pulley) — 3 × 12–15 @ 0–1 RIR · alternate arms**
- **Targets:** upper pec ▸ front delts
- **Why:** Sweeping from low-and-behind to high-and-across follows the upper-pec fibers. The low cable keeps tension at the start, with your arm down and back and the pec stretched.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics, plus Chaves 2020 for the upper-pec rationale.
- **Cues:** Stand side-on, a step from the pulley, with your arm starting slightly behind your hip and a slight fixed bend in your elbow. Sweep up and across to chin height, then take 2–3 s back to the stretch. **Alternative:** incline DB flye at 30° if the footplate gets in the way.

**B4. Lean-Away DB Lateral Raise — 5 × 12–20 @ 0–1 RIR · alternate arms**
- **Targets:** side delts
- **Why:** Leaning 15–20° away from the working arm shifts where the load is zero, so the bottom of the range, where the delt is stretched, carries load. It still loads the mid-range. This gives a different resistance curve from Monday's cable raise.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Hold the rack upright with your free hand and lean away. Raise to shoulder height slightly in front of your body. On the last set, add 5–8 bottom-half partials after you fail at full range.

**B5. Lying DB Overhead Extension (neutral grip, lowered behind the head) — 4 × 8–12 @ 1 RIR (0 on the last set) · rest 2 min**
- **Targets:** triceps long head ▸ medial and lateral heads
- **Why:** Angling your upper arms back toward your head keeps the long head stretched, the same principle as in Maeo 2023, while letting you use heavier loads than the cable version. Neutral-grip dumbbells are easier on wrists and elbows than a straight bar.
- **Evidence:** Same stretched-triceps mechanism as Maeo 2023, used here with heavier loads.
- **Cues:** Lie on a flat bench with the DBs above your forehead and your arms angled back. Lower the DBs beside and behind your head into a deep elbow bend, keeping your upper arms still, then extend fully. "Failure" here means one last controlled rep, not a grind with weight over your face.

## PULL B (Friday)

**D1. Single-Arm DB Row (knee and hand on bench) — 3 × 8–12 per arm @ 1–2 RIR · ~60 s between arms**
- **Targets:** lats ▸ teres major, mid traps, rear delts, biceps
- **Why:** On one arm, your shoulder can drop forward and your torso can rotate slightly at the bottom, which stretches the lat further than a two-arm row. The braced torso keeps lower-back fatigue low the day before RDLs.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Keep a flat back and let the DB hang so your shoulder drops toward the floor. Pull in an arc toward your hip pocket, not straight up. Straps are fine.

**D2. Wide-Grip Seated Cable Row (straight bar) — 3 × 10–12 @ 1 RIR · rest 2 min**
- **Targets:** mid/upper traps, rhomboids, rear delts ▸ lats
- **Why:** Flaring your elbows 60–75° shifts the work from the lats to the upper back and rear delts. It complements C3 and D1, which are lat-focused.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Pull to your lower chest with elbows out, squeeze your shoulder blades together, and reach forward fully at the stretch.

**D3. Half-Kneeling Single-Arm Lat Pulldown (D-handle) — 3 × 10–12 per arm @ 1 RIR · ~60 s between arms**
- **Targets:** lats, including the lowest fibers ▸ teres major
- **Why:** One arm can reach higher and slightly across your body, which lengthens the lat more at the top. You can also pull along the line of the fibers, down toward your hip.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Reach up and across, start each rep by pulling your shoulder blade down, and drive your elbow to the side of your hip. A slight side-bend toward the working side at the bottom is fine.

**D4. Chest-Supported Reverse DB Fly — 3 × 12–20 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 90 s**
- **Targets:** rear delts ▸ mid traps
- **Why:** It's direct rear-delt work with no momentum. Dumbbells load the shortened end of the range, which complements the face pull's stretched-position loading.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Set the bench to 30° with a slight bend in your elbows. Sweep your arms out to torso height, not higher, and keep your shoulder blades fairly still so the rear delts do the work.

**D5. DB Preacher Curl (arm over the incline backrest) — 3 × 8–12 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 90 s**
- **Targets:** biceps (short head emphasis) ▸ brachialis
- **Why:** With your upper arm braced in front of you, load peaks near the bottom, where the biceps is stretched. The brace also removes cheating.
- **Evidence:** Pedrosa 2023 used exactly this exercise. Training the stretched half produced more lower-biceps growth and larger 1-rep max gains.
- **Cues:** Set the backrest high (60–70°) with your armpit snug over the top. Lower to nearly full extension without bouncing, and stop about 20° before vertical at the top. On the last set, add lengthened partials.

**D6. Rope Hammer Curl (low pulley) — 2 × 10–15 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 60–90 s**
- **Targets:** brachialis, brachioradialis ▸ biceps
- **Why:** A thumbs-up grip takes away the biceps' advantage and shifts work to the brachialis and brachioradialis, which add arm thickness.
- **Evidence:** Mostly biomechanics and EMG. It's the weakest-evidence pick in the program, kept as cheap insurance.
- **Cues:** Keep your elbows pinned and step back so the cable is angled at the bottom.

## LEGS B (Saturday)

**F1. Romanian Deadlift — 4 × 6–10 @ 2 RIR · rest 3 min**
- **Targets:** hamstrings (biceps femoris long head, semimembranosus) ▸ glutes, adductor magnus, lower back
- **Why:** Hips bent with knees nearly straight puts the hamstrings at long lengths. Torque peaks at the bottom, where the bar is farthest from your hips, so the lift naturally loads the stretch.
- **Evidence:** Hip extension beat Nordics for growing the biceps femoris long head (Bourne 2017). Hamstrings grow more when trained at longer lengths (Maeo 2021).
- **Cues:** Fix a slight knee bend, push your hips back, and keep the bar against your thighs. Descend to your stretch limit with a neutral spine, usually about mid-shin. Use straps.

**F2. Heels-Elevated Front Squat — 3 × 8–12 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 2.5–3 min**
- **Targets:** quads ▸ glutes, adductors
- **Why:** The front-loaded bar and raised heels keep your torso upright and let your knees travel further forward. That shifts the work to the quads and away from the hips, which RDLs already hit today. It's also a different stimulus from Wednesday's back squat.
- **Evidence:** Varying knee-extension exercises grew more parts of the quads than squatting alone (Fonseca 2014).
- **Cues:** Put 1–2" plates or a wedge under your heels. Keep your elbows high, let your knees go forward, and squat to full depth. **Swap:** heels-elevated goblet squat if front-rack mobility limits you, though your heaviest dumbbell then caps the load.

**F3. Hip-Flexed Prone DB Leg Curl — 3 × 10–15 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 90 s**
- **Targets:** all knee-bending hamstring heads, including the short head of the biceps femoris ▸ calves (gastrocnemius)
- **Why:** Lying face-down with your chest on the inclined backrest bends your hips, which lengthens the hamstrings the way a seated leg curl does. Load peaks when your shin is horizontal, near full extension, so the stretched position is loaded.
- **Evidence:** Seated leg curls, with the hips bent, produced substantially more hamstring growth than lying curls over 12 weeks (Maeo 2021, MRI).
- **Cues:**
  - Set the backrest to about 30°, put your hips at the seat-backrest junction, and let your knees hang off the end of the seat.
  - Have someone hand you the DB to grip between your feet.
  - **Alternative:** an ankle cuff on the low pulley, if you own one.
  - Curl to about 90°, then take 3 s to lower to full extension.

**F4. Sissy Squat (holding a plate) — 3 × 8–15 @ 1 RIR · rest 90 s**
- **Targets:** quads, especially rectus femoris
- **Why:** You have no leg-extension machine, so this is your quad isolation. Your hips stay straight while your knees bend deeply, so the rectus femoris is stretched at both joints. It's the one quad muscle that crosses the hip, and squats load it at a relatively short length because bending the hip shortens it.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics, based on the long-length trials above. There's no direct sissy-squat hypertrophy trial.
- **Cues:** Hold the rack upright and rise onto the balls of your feet. Push your knees forward while keeping a straight line from knees to shoulders, and descend until your hamstrings meet your calves. **Easier version:** reverse Nordic (kneeling).

**F5. Seated Calf Raise — 3 × 12–20 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 60 s**
- **Targets:** soleus
- **Why:** Bent knees slacken the gastrocnemius and shift the work to the soleus, which straight-knee raises under-train.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics.
- **Cues:** Sit with your toes on a plate and a padded barbell or heavy DBs on your lower thighs. Pause 2 s at the bottom.

**F6. Standing Calf Raise, bottom-half partials — 3 × 12–20 @ 0–1 RIR · rest 60 s**
- **Targets:** gastrocnemius
- **Why:** It applies Kassiano 2023 directly: each rep runs only from the full stretch to a neutral ankle.
- **Evidence:** Kassiano 2023.
- **Cues:** Stand on both feet on a plate stack with a DB in one hand and the rack in the other.

**Optional:** cable crunches (rope, high pulley) for 2–3 × 10–15 on both pull days. Hip thrusts aren't required, because squats grew the glutes about as well (Plotkin 2023).

# 4. Progression & deload protocol

## Double progression (every exercise)

1. **Starting load:** pick the weight that puts set 1 at the bottom of the rep range at the target RIR.
2. **Add reps first.** Each week, add at least 1 rep to at least one set at the same load and RIR, with the same range of motion and tempo. Shortened reps don't count.
3. **Then add load.** When every working set reaches the top of the range at the target RIR, increase the weight the next week:
   - Squat and RDL: +5 kg (10 lb)
   - Presses and barbell rows: +2.5 kg (5 lb)
   - Dumbbells: the next dumbbell up
   - Cable: the next pin

   Reps will drop back to the bottom of the range; build them back up.
4. **Big-jump rule.** If the next jump is more than about 10% (for example 20 → 25 lb on laterals is +25%), extend the range first by going 3–5 reps past the top, then jump. Adding reps and adding load produced similar growth (Plotkin 2022).
5. **Stall rule.** If an exercise gains no reps or load for 2 weeks in a row, check sleep and food first. Then cut that exercise by 1 set. If it's still stuck, swap the variation in the next block.

## Block structure (5 loading weeks + 1 deload)

| Week | Compound RIR | Isolation RIR | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 2 | Find working loads. Nordics 2 × 3–5. Take the last set of one isolation per session to true failure to calibrate your RIR estimates. |
| 2 | 2 | 1 | Full prescribed sets |
| 3 | 1–2 | 0–1 | Normal progression |
| 4 | 1–2 | 0–1 | Optional +1 set for 1–2 lagging muscles, only with no fatigue flags |
| 5 | 1 (squat/RDL 1–2) | 0, with partials on the last set | Hardest week |
| 6 | Deload | | See below |

**Extending to 8 weeks:** if you're still progressing at the end of week 5 with no more than 1 fatigue flag, repeat week 5's RIRs in weeks 6–7 and deload in week 8. Never run more than 7 loading weeks.

## Fatigue flags (check weekly)
- Reps dropped at the same load on 2 or more exercises, and it wasn't just one bad day
- Joint or tendon pain that lasts into your warm-ups
- A muscle is still sore when it's due to be trained again
- Worse sleep, higher resting heart rate, or flat motivation

**1 flag:** hold steady that week (don't push for extra reps or load). **2 or more flags for a week:** deload now, whatever week it is.

## Deload week
- Keep the same exercises and days.
- Halve the sets (3 → 2, 4 → 2, 5 → 2–3).
- Keep loads the same or drop them 10%, and stay at 3–4 RIR on everything.
- No failure, no partials, and Nordics at 1 × 3 at most.

**Why cut volume instead of taking the week off:** a full week off in the middle of a 9-week program didn't change muscle growth but did blunt strength gains (Coleman 2024). A Delphi consensus of coaches describes deloads as planned cuts, mainly to volume and effort, every 4–8 weeks to manage fatigue (Bell 2023). The goal is to clear fatigue and protect your joints, not to trigger a growth rebound.

## Next block
- Keep 70–80% of the exercises and rotate one variation per muscle. For example, swap which day gets incline DB vs incline barbell, change the split squat to a deficit reverse lunge, or switch to weighted pull-ups if you have a bar.
- Restart week 1 at about 90–95% of your final loads, using week 1's RIRs.

**Nutrition:** none of this works without enough food. Aim for about 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight (Morton 2018) and eat at maintenance or in a small surplus.

**Limits of the evidence:** most of these trials are short (8–12 weeks), small, and often in untrained people. The program reflects the current best evidence, not certainty.

## New exercise breakdowns

**Weighted Dip — 2 × 6–12 @ 1–2 RIR · rest 2.5–3 min**
- **Targets:** lower and mid pec ▸ triceps, front delts
- **Why:** Load on the shoulder is highest at the bottom, where your shoulders are most extended and the pec is most stretched. That makes dips a heavy compound that loads the stretch, like a press and a flye combined. They're easy to load with a belt and put no strain on your lower back.
- **Evidence:** Biomechanics only; no trial has compared dips with pressing for growth. The rationale is the long-length research (Maeo 2021/2023, Pedrosa 2023). Deep dips put a lot of strain on the front of the shoulder, so depth is limited by comfort, not by a rule.
- **Cues:**
  - Lean your torso 20–30° forward with elbows flared slightly.
  - Take 2 s to lower until your upper arms are just below parallel, or until you feel a strong chest stretch without pain at the front of the shoulder. Don't bounce.
  - Add a dip belt or a DB between your feet once you can do 12 or more bodyweight reps. Use a band for help if bodyweight is too hard.

**EZ-Bar Preacher Curl (arms over the incline backrest) — 3 × 8–12 @ 0–1 RIR**
- **Targets:** biceps (short head emphasis) ▸ brachialis
- **Why:** The mechanics are the same as the DB version: with your upper arms braced in front of you, the load peaks near the bottom, where the biceps is stretched. The angled grip is easier on wrists and elbows than a straight bar, and doing both arms at once saves time on the busiest day.
- **Evidence:** Pedrosa 2023 used the preacher curl. Training the stretched half produced more growth in the lower biceps.
- **Cues:** Set the backrest to 60–70° with your armpits snug over the top. Lower to nearly full extension under control without bouncing, and stop about 20° before vertical at the top. On the last set, add lengthened partials.

**EZ-Bar Skull Crusher, lowered behind the head — 3 × 8–12 @ 1 RIR (0 on the last set)**
- **Targets:** triceps long head ▸ medial and lateral heads
- **Why:** Tilting your upper arms back 20–30° toward your head and lowering the bar *behind* it raises your arms further overhead. That stretches the long head, the same principle as Maeo 2023's overhead result, but with heavier loads than the cable version.
- **Evidence:** It applies the mechanism from Maeo 2023. Pairing it with preacher curls as a superset doesn't cost growth: a 2025 meta-analysis found supersets produced similar hypertrophy and strength to regular sets in less time, and pairing opposing muscles even allowed more reps (Zhang 2025).
- **Cues:** Lie on a flat bench with a close grip on the angled part of the bar. Keep your upper arms tilted back and lower the bar behind your head into a deep elbow bend. Extend to just short of lockout while keeping that tilt, so tension stays on, and don't let your elbows flare.
