# Evidence-Based Exercise Selection: Lower Body, Core, and the Main Strength Lifts

Scope note: 17 searches, no full-text fetches. Most findings come from abstracts, PubMed/PMC landing pages, and evidence-based practitioner summaries (Stronger By Science, Biolayne, BarBend). Confidence labels: **High** = several longitudinal trials or a meta-analysis agree; **Moderate** = one or two decent longitudinal trials; **Low** = a single small trial, EMG/acute data only, or practitioner opinion. Tier rankings (S/A/B/C) are **inferences** that combine cited findings with mechanistic reasoning. They are not direct outputs of any study.

Context for the report writer: the generator currently picks sumo deadlift and pause squat as the main hypertrophy leg lifts, uses cable hip abduction/adduction and supermans as slot fillers, and programs farmer carries. The evidence below explains why each of those is a poor default for a hypertrophy goal.

## Q1. Hamstrings: seated vs lying leg curl, and RDL (hip extension) vs curls (knee flexion)

### Takeaway
Seated leg curls grew the hamstrings more than prone (lying) curls, because the flexed hip lengthens the biarticular hamstrings (Moderate). No longitudinal head-to-head of RDL vs leg curl was found. Indirect data suggest the two load different regions (curls favor biceps femoris and semitendinosus; hip-hinge favors semimembranosus), so the best default is one hinge plus one curl (Low–Moderate).

### Cited Findings
- Maeo et al. 2021 (MSSE): 20 young adults, within-subject, 12 weeks, one leg seated curl and the other prone curl. Whole-hamstring volume rose about 14% (seated) vs about 9% (prone). Semitendinosus, semimembranosus, and biceps femoris long head grew more with seated curls; the short head (monoarticular) did not differ. **Moderate** — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [PMC7969179](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179)
- Some secondary coverage overstates this as "50% more growth." The whole-muscle gap was about 5 percentage points — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/leg-day-hack-hamstring-gains/) (practitioner media)
- EMG: the leg curl produced higher overall hamstring activation than the stiff-leg deadlift, partly because the biceps femoris short head works only in knee flexion. **Low** (EMG, not growth) — [Breaking Muscle summary of Contreras/Schoenfeld](https://breakingmuscle.com/study-compares-hamstring-muscle-activation-during-the-deadlift-and-leg-curl/)
- The lying leg curl gave the highest hamstring activation and hamstring-to-quad EMG ratio of the exercises tested; the RDL was also relatively high. **Low** — [PubMed 38006325](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38006325/)
- 2025 blinded RCT, untrained participants: Nordic curl vs stiff-leg deadlift produced similar whole-hamstring hypertrophy. The stiff-leg deadlift group showed selective semimembranosus growth. **Moderate** (single RCT) — [PubMed 35989703 / related](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35989703/) (identity of this PubMed ID vs the 2025 RCT not verified; see Gaps)
- Muscle-force and fascicle modelling: the Nordic produced more fascicle lengthening; the Roman chair and single-leg deadlift may better load the long head and semimembranosus. **Low** — [PubMed 35307884](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35307884/)
- Kubo 2019: full and half squats produced no hamstring growth. Squats are not a hamstring exercise. **Moderate** — [Springer EJAP](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y)
- Plotkin 2023: similarly, squats and hip thrusts did not build hamstrings much. The paper reported gluteal, quadriceps, and adductor growth. **Moderate** — [Frontiers in Physiology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)

### Inferences
- Encode the hamstrings as needing **both** a knee-flexion movement and a hip-extension (hinge) movement. Squats and leg presses should give near-zero hamstring credit.
- Seated curl > lying curl as the default curl; lying curl is a fine second-choice or variety option.
- RDL/SLDL is the preferred hinge for hypertrophy over the conventional deadlift. It keeps the hamstrings under load at long length, needs no floor start, and is easier to progress in a moderate rep range. Its fatigue cost is lower than a conventional pull. (Mechanistic inference.)

| 1 | Seated leg curl | S | High | High (machine) | High | High | Low | Low | Maeo 2021 |
| 2 | Romanian deadlift (barbell/DB) | S/A | High | Moderate | Moderate | High | Moderate | Low back moderate | Hinge slot default |

### Gaps
- No longitudinal RDL vs leg curl hypertrophy trial was found.
- The 2025 Nordic vs SLDL RCT appeared only as a search snippet; the exact citation (authors/journal) was not verified.

## Q2. Quadriceps: squat depth, leg extension and the rectus femoris, hack squat, leg press, Bulgarian split squat

### Takeaway
Deep squats are not clearly better than half squats for the quads, but they are better for the glutes and adductors and for full-squat strength (Moderate). The rectus femoris grows poorly from squats and leg press; it needs knee extensions, ideally with the hip less flexed (reclined) (Moderate). Training the knee extension in its lengthened (deep-knee-flexion) range produces more growth than the short range (Moderate).

### Cited Findings
- Kubo, Ikebukuro & Yata 2019 (EJAP): 17 men, 10 weeks, full squat (~140° knee flexion) vs half squat (~90°). Quad growth was similar. Adductor and gluteus maximus growth was significantly greater with full squats (p = 0.026 and 0.008). Full-squat 1RM rose 31.8% vs 11.3%. **Moderate** (small n) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y); [Stronger By Science summary](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/squat-depth/)
- Pallarés et al. (2019/2020, often cited as 2021): 53 trained men, full vs parallel vs half squat. Full squat gave greater gains in full, parallel, and half squat 1RM, and less pain. No hypertrophy outcome was found in the sources reviewed. **Moderate** for strength — [PubMed 31092132](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31092132/); [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/?p=250628)
- A systematic review on ROM and hypertrophy (Wolf et al.): training at longer muscle lengths, through partial or full ROM, tends to produce optimal growth for muscles including the quadriceps. **Moderate** — [PubMed 36662126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662126/)
- Pedrosa et al. 2022 (EJSS): 45 untrained women, knee extension. Initial-range partials (100°–65°, the long-length range) produced greater relative hypertrophy in some regions than final-range partials or full ROM. **Moderate** — [PubMed via katalog.lib.cas.cz record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,33977835); [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Gustavo-Pedrosa/publication/351538891_Partial_range_of_motion_training_elicits_favorable_improvements_in_muscular_adaptations_when_carried_out_at_long_muscle_lengths/links/61f1e6bbc5e3103375c2e9f9/Partial-range-of-motion-training-elicits-favorable-improvements-in-muscular-adaptations-when-carried-out-at-long-muscle-lengths.pdf)
- Hip angle in leg extension: Larsen et al. 2025 (J Sports Sci 43(2):210–221; preprint SportRxiv 407), 22 untrained men, 10 weeks. 40° hip flexion (reclined) grew the rectus femoris more than 90° (upright seated). There was strong evidence of **no** difference for the vastus lateralis. Note: this is Larsen et al., not Maeo. The "Maeo 2023" in the brief appears to be a mix-up with Maeo's triceps paper (overhead > pushdown), which shows the same stretch principle. **Moderate** — [PubMed 39699974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39699974/); [SportRxiv](https://doi.org/10.51224/SRXIV.407)
- Mitsuya et al. 2023: in bodybuilders, 40° hip flexion gave higher proximal/mid rectus femoris activation than 80°. **Low** (EMG) — [Search record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_2777404553)
- Single- vs multi-joint, 12 weeks: rectus femoris volume rose +13.2% with knee extension vs +1.1% with leg press. Leg press matched knee extension for vasti and whole quads, and also grew the gluteus maximus (~15%) and adductor magnus, which knee extension did not. **Moderate** — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/hypertrophic_effects_of_single__versus_multi_joint.1002-1.pdf); [Biolayne summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-51/leg-press-or-knee-extension-for-quad-growth/)
- Squat vs leg extension: squats favored distal vastus lateralis growth; leg extension favored the rectus femoris. **Low–Moderate** (cited via a search summary) — [Biolayne](https://biolayne.com/?p=219456)
- Squat vs leg press: squat training transferred better to squat 1RM than leg press training. No hypertrophy outcome reported. **Moderate** — [PubMed 27735888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27735888/)
- Squat vs leg press hypertrophy: no published head-to-head was found; an 8-week RCT is registered without results — [ISRCTN](https://www.isrctn.com/editorial/retrieveFile/8a4fead9-6735-499a-8120-67c15f6f35ec/47628)
- Bulgarian split squat: biomechanics show it is relatively hip-dominant vs the back squat, with a higher hamstring-to-quad EMG ratio. Modeled forces for glute max, vasti, and biceps femoris long head rose more with load in split squats and step-ups than in squats (Kipp 2022, via blog). **Low** — [Georgia Southern](https://scholars.georgiasouthern.edu/en/publications/biomechanical-differences-between-the-bulgarian-split-squat-and-b/); [PMC8136570](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8136570)
- Hack squat: no longitudinal hypertrophy trial was found.

### Inferences
- Model the quads as two parts. **Vasti** are served by any deep knee-dominant compound (squat, hack squat, leg press, split squat). The **rectus femoris** needs a knee extension, ideally reclined.
- For a hypertrophy goal, the "main" quad compound should be a high-stability, deep-knee-flexion movement: hack squat, leg press, or a high-bar/front squat to full depth. A **pause squat** is a strength/technique variation. It adds no clear hypertrophy benefit, reduces the loads you can use, and should not be the default hypertrophy main lift.
- Depth should be encoded as "full depth preferred." Half-squat variants rank lower.

**Quadriceps ranked (hypertrophy)**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hack squat (machine), full depth | S | High | High | High | High | Low | Knees moderate | Practitioner consensus; no RCT |
| 2 | Back squat (high-bar), full depth | S/A | High | Moderate | Moderate | High | High | Moderate; higher systemic fatigue | Also glutes/adductors (Kubo 2019) |
| 3 | Leg press (45°/horizontal), deep | A/S | Moderate–High | High | High | High | Low | Low (watch lumbar flexion at bottom) | Also glute max and adductor magnus |
| 4 | Leg extension (reclined / hip ~40°) | S for rectus femoris, A overall | High (if loaded deep) | High | Very high | High | Low | Low–moderate knee | Only exercise that reliably grows rectus femoris |
| 5 | Front squat | A | High | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | High | Moderate | |

### Gaps
- No longitudinal trial on hack squat vs back squat vs leg press for regional quad growth.
- No Maeo 2023 leg-extension hip-angle paper was found; the evidence is Larsen et al. 2025.
- Pallarés hypertrophy outcomes (if any) were not retrieved.

## Q3. Glutes: hip thrust vs squat (Plotkin 2023, Barbalho 2020), abductors

### Takeaway
Hip thrusts and squats produce similar gluteus maximus growth in untrained people. Squats additionally grow the quads and adductors (Moderate). Full-depth squats and lengthened-position hip extension appear better for glutes than shortened-range work (Low–Moderate). Gluteus medius/minimus growth from typical training is small and poorly documented (Low).

### Cited Findings
- Plotkin et al. 2023 (Frontiers in Physiology): untrained adults, 9 weeks, hip thrust (n = 18) vs squat (n = 16), MRI. Gluteal growth was similar (hip thrust slightly ahead, with wide CIs). Squats produced more quad and adductor growth. Hip thrust had higher gluteal EMG, which did not predict hypertrophy. Gluteus medius + minimus showed little to no growth in either group. **Moderate** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full); [PMC10593473](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10593473/)
- Barbalho et al. 2020 (Int J Sports Med 41(5):306–310): trained women, 12 weeks. Glute max thickness rose 9.4% (squat) vs 3.7% (hip thrust), and squat 1RM rose 35.9% vs 4.3%. This finding was **contested**: Bret Contreras critiqued the strength data, and a 2025 meta-analysis flagged insufficient methodological detail. **Low–Moderate** — [Thieme](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/a-1082-1126); [Contreras critique (practitioner)](https://bretcontreras.com/wp-content/uploads/Squats-Don%E2%80%99t-Beat-Out-Hip-Thrusts-for-Building-the-Glutes-Matheus-Barbalho-Paulo-Gentil-Have-Some-Serious-Explaining-to-Do-to-the-Sports-Science-Community.pdf); [2025 meta-analysis, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1542334/full)
- Kubo 2019: full squats grew the glute max significantly more than half squats. **Moderate** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y)
- Lengthened partials produced about twice the hip-extensor growth of full ROM in one study (+6.8% vs +3.1%). **Low** (single study, via practitioner summary) — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/)
- Leg press grew the glute max by about 15% over 12 weeks. **Moderate** — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/hypertrophic_effects_of_single__versus_multi_joint.1002-1.pdf)
- Gluteus medius: in a hip OA RCT, targeted training grew gluteus minimus volume but not gluteus medius. In hip-related groin pain, standard strength/flexibility training increased gluteus medius volume modestly. **Low** — [BMC Musculoskelet Disord](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12891-022-05907-4); [PMC9617418](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9617418)

### Inferences
- Glute max needs no isolation work if the program includes deep squats/leg press plus a hinge. A hip thrust or glute-focused exercise is an **A-tier** option for glute-priority users, not a required slot.
- Cable hip abduction is a low-value filler for general hypertrophy goals. Glute medius growth evidence is weak, and the cable version has poor stability and is hard to progress. If abduction is included, a seated machine abduction is the more practical choice (higher stability, easy loading). This preference is mechanistic, not trial-tested.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deep squat (back/hack/front) | S | High | Moderate–High | Moderate–High | High | Moderate | Kubo 2019; Plotkin 2023 |
| 2 | Barbell / machine hip thrust | A/S | Low–Moderate (peak load at short length) | High | High | High | Low | Equal to squat for glutes (Plotkin) |
| 3 | Bulgarian split squat / deficit reverse lunge | A | High | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Hip-dominant |
| 4 | Deep leg press (feet high) | A | Moderate–High | High | High | High | Low | |
| 5 | Romanian deadlift | A | High | Moderate | Moderate | High | Moderate | |
| 6 | 45° back extension (glute-biased) | B | High | High | High | Moderate | Low | |
| 7 | Seated machine hip abduction (glute med/min) | B | Low–Moderate | High | High | High | Low | Optional |
| 8 | Cable kickback / cable abduction | C | Variable | Low | Moderate | Low | Moderate | Avoid as default slot filler |

### Gaps
- No longitudinal trials on hip abduction exercise and glute medius hypertrophy in healthy trained people were found.

## Q4. Adductors

### Takeaway
The adductors (especially adductor magnus, which is a hip extensor) grow from deep squats and leg press. Most programs therefore cover them without isolation work (Moderate).

### Cited Findings
- Full squats grew the adductors significantly more than half squats (Kubo 2019). Stronger By Science notes that the adductor longus is a secondary hip flexor, so the result mostly reflects the adductor magnus. **Moderate** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y); [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/squats-adductors/)
- Squats grew the adductors more than hip thrusts (Plotkin 2023). **Moderate** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- Leg press grew the adductor magnus; knee extension did not. **Moderate** — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/hypertrophic_effects_of_single__versus_multi_joint.1002-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Give deep squats, leg press, and wide-stance/sumo patterns substantial adductor credit. Isolation adduction (machine) is a **B-tier** optional accessory, not a required slot. **Cable adduction** is a C: low stability, awkward setup, hard to progress.
- **Adductors ranked:** (1) deep back/front squat — S; (2) deep leg press — A; (3) seated adduction machine — B; (4) Copenhagen plank — B (injury prevention, hard to progress); (5) sumo deadlift — B (adductor-heavy but fatiguing); (6) cable adduction — C.

### Gaps
- No direct longitudinal trial on isolated adduction training for hypertrophy was found.

## Q5. Calves: long-length emphasis (Kassiano 2023)

### Takeaway
Calf raises emphasizing the stretched (dorsiflexed) range produce more gastrocnemius growth than full ROM, which beats short-range partials (Moderate). Straight-knee raises are needed to load the gastrocnemius; bent-knee (seated) raises shift load to the soleus (Low; anatomical reasoning, not trial evidence here).

### Cited Findings
- Kassiano et al. 2023 (JSCR 37(9):1746–1753): 42 young women, 8 weeks, 3×/week. Lengthened partials (−25° to 0°) > full ROM > shortened partials (0° to +25°) for gastrocnemius thickness. **Moderate** — [PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/); [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=60530)
- SBS reports the lengthened-partial result as roughly double: lateral gastrocnemius +7.3% vs +14.9%, medial +6.7% vs +15.2%. **Moderate** — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/)
- A 2025 trained-lifter study found a small, uncertain advantage for initial-range partials over past-failure partials. **Low** (preprint) — [SportRxiv](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/542/1162/1086)
- Milo Wolf (practitioner/researcher) expects about 5–10% extra growth from lengthened partials overall, not a doubling. **Low** (expert opinion) — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/long-length-partial-reps/)

### Inferences
- Encode calf exercises with a "deep stretch with pause" cue. **Calves ranked:** (1) standing calf raise machine, deep stretch — S; (2) leg press calf raise — S/A; (3) Smith machine calf raise on a step — A; (4) seated calf raise (soleus) — A as complement; (5) single-leg DB calf raise on a step — B (stability limits load); (6) flat-floor calf raises without a step — C (no stretch); farmer carries/jumping as calf "work" — C.

### Gaps
- Few calf trials in trained men; Kassiano was in young women.

## Q6. Lower back / erectors

### Takeaway
No trial of erector hypertrophy from deadlifts vs back extensions was found. Erectors get substantial work from squats, hinges, and rows, so isolated low-back work is optional (Low). The Romanian deadlift did not improve isolated lumbar extension torque, but isolated lumbar extension training improved both lumbar torque and RDL 1RM (Moderate, single RCT).

### Cited Findings
- RCT, 36 trained men, 10 weeks: RDL training did not increase lumbar extension torque; isolated lumbar extension training improved lumbar torque and RDL 1RM. **Moderate** — [PubMed 23867152](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867152/)
- Neither back squat nor hip thrust training meaningfully increased isolated lumbar extension strength. **Low–Moderate** — [PeerJ 7337](https://peerj.com/articles/7337)
- The conventional deadlift showed high erector EMG (73% MVIC vs 53% for a machine variation). **Low** (EMG) — [PubMed 27893476](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27893476/)

### Inferences
- Supermans (prone floor extensions) are a C: tiny load, short ROM, nearly impossible to progress. A 45° back extension (loaded with a plate) is the better isolation choice (B/A).
- **Erectors ranked:** (1) 45°/90° back extension, loaded — A; (2) RDL — A (counts toward hamstrings too); (3) conventional deadlift — B (great stimulus, high fatigue); (4) good morning — B; (5) reverse hyper — B; (6) superman / bird-dog — C (rehab/warm-up only).

### Gaps
- No longitudinal erector hypertrophy comparisons were found.

## Q7. Abs and obliques: crunch variants vs planks; is direct work needed?

### Takeaway
Dynamic flexion exercises (crunches, cable crunches) produce more rectus abdominis shortening/thickening than leg raises, and planks mostly train stability, not size (Low–Moderate). Upper vs lower ab variants do not produce regionally different growth (Low). Longitudinal hypertrophy data on direct ab training are sparse.

### Cited Findings
- 2023 ultrasound study: rectus abdominis shortening and thickening were largest during the crunch vs leg raises. **Low** (acute) — [PMC10824285](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10824285/)
- 9-week trial in trained women (thesis): infra- vs supra-abdominal exercise did not produce regionally different rectus abdominis hypertrophy. **Low** — [UFMG repository](https://repositorio.ufmg.br//bitstreams/ab000156-31a6-433f-8329-250395b2300a/download)
- Acute crunch study (15 trained adults, 4×10RM vs 1×40RM) measured thickness but over a single session only. **Low** — [PMC10446947](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10446947)
- The ACE review of 15 ab exercises found no real difference between upper and lower ab emphasis; the plank engages the transverse abdominis. **Low** (EMG, practitioner) — [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/research-special-issue-2015/5318/abs-abs-abs/)
- The stability-ball crunch (ball low) produced more rectus abdominis/external oblique activity than the floor crunch. **Low** (EMG) — [PubMed 17530978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17530978/)

### Inferences
- If abs are trained for size, use **loaded, progressive flexion** with some stretch: cable crunch (S/A), decline/weighted crunch (A), stability-ball crunch with extension (A), hanging knee/leg raise (B; hip-flexor dominant unless the pelvis curls), ab wheel rollout (A/B; long-length anti-extension, hard to progress). Obliques: cable woodchop / side-bend / Pallof press (B).
- **Planks** are a C for hypertrophy: little progression path besides time. Keep them for warm-up, beginners, or rehab.
- Whether direct ab work is needed: no longitudinal comparison of compounds-only vs compounds plus direct ab work was found. An algorithm should treat 1–2 sets of a flexion exercise per session as optional.

### Gaps
- No good longitudinal trial on whether compound lifts alone grow the rectus abdominis.
- No oblique hypertrophy trials were found.

## Q8. Fatigue per unit stimulus (deadlifts, farmer carries, etc.) and how often to include them

### Takeaway
Volume-equated deadlift, squat, and bench recovered on similar time courses (48–72 h) in one study, so the "deadlifts fry your CNS" claim is not well supported (Low–Moderate). Even so, the conventional deadlift and sumo deadlift give modest hypertrophy stimulus to any one muscle relative to their total fatigue. Practitioners and expert consensus favor placing heavy compounds first and using machines and isolation work for most failure-close volume (Low–Moderate, consensus).

### Cited Findings
- Belcher et al. 2019: 4 sets to failure at 80% 1RM, volume equated. Soreness and damage markers recovered within 48–72 h for all three lifts; the authors judged the deadlift can be trained at similar frequency to the squat and bench. **Low–Moderate** — [CSEP summary](https://csep.ca/2019/06/19/time-course-of-recovery-is-similar-for-the-back-squat-bench-press-and-deadlift-in-well-trained-males-2/)
- A newer study found the deadlift produced smaller but more persistent reductions in braking rate of force development than the back squat. **Low** (abstract only) — [IRIS UniPA PDF](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/b7a2b898-014e-4d15-b54f-7245a05bad8c/ldag013.pdf)
- Schoenfeld's expert consensus suggests limiting failure training mainly to single-joint and machine exercises to manage the stimulus-to-fatigue ratio. **Low** (consensus) — [NSCA slides](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/events/pdf/nat22/schoenfeld-brad.pdf)
- "Stimulus-to-fatigue ratio" is a practitioner concept (popularized by Israetel/RP; Nippard). An exercise has a high SFR when it strongly works the target muscle with little local or systemic fatigue. It is not a validated measurement. **Low** — [Explainer](https://www.aol.com/articles/stimulus-fatigue-ratio-explained-choose-011339752.html)
- No Henselmans source on deadlift SFR was found.

### Inferences
- Give each exercise a **systemic_fatigue** score (1–5). Suggested values: conventional/sumo deadlift 5, back squat 4, good morning 4, front squat 4, RDL 3, Bulgarian split squat 3, hack squat 3, leg press 2–3, hip thrust 2, machine isolation 1. For hypertrophy goals, cap at **one** fatigue-5 exercise per week (or none). Never pick two fatigue-4/5 lower-body compounds as the main lifts of the same session.
- Farmer carries: grip, traps, and conditioning tool; little hypertrophy evidence for any lower-body muscle. Tag as "conditioning/GPP," C-tier for hypertrophy, and exclude from hypertrophy accessory slots unless the user opts in.

### Gaps
- No direct hypertrophy-per-fatigue measurement exists for any exercise; SFR scores are expert judgment.

## Q9. Strength and powerlifting goals: specificity of competition lifts and the role of variations

### Takeaway
Strength gains are most specific to the trained lift. Transfer between similar lifts is real but smaller and often asymmetric (Moderate). Powerlifters overwhelmingly use the competition lifts plus planned variations (Low, survey). Full-depth squatting builds strength across depths better than partials (Moderate).

### Cited Findings
- Plotkin 2023: each group gained most on its own trained lift (squat 3RM favored the squat group; hip thrust 3RM favored the hip thrust group); deadlift gains were similar. **Moderate** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- Squat-only training transferred better to squat 1RM than leg press training. **Moderate** — [PubMed 27735888](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27735888/)
- Appleby et al. 2019: 18 weeks of back squat vs step-up. Each group improved most on its trained exercise, with smaller transfer to the other. **Moderate** — [QxMD record](https://read.qxmd.com/read/30688873/specificity-and-transfer-of-lower-body-strength-influence-of-bilateral-or-unilateral-lower-body-resistance-training)
- NSCA review (2022) on transfer between lifts: transfer occurs but varies by exercise and is asymmetric (e.g., front squat training raised hip thrust 3RM more than hip thrust training raised front squat). **Moderate** — [NSCA SCJ PDF](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/3c0357cb66b54c6b9a598b0ca578600a/scj-transfer-between-lifts-2022.pdf)
- Monte et al. 2026: squat training raised leg extension 1RM ~16%; leg extension training raised squat 1RM only ~9%. **Low–Moderate** — [Univr PDF](https://iris.univr.it/retrieve/7b2c5c60-2a23-4838-8a39-cbb3fc5aa271/s00421-026-06391-7.pdf)
- Amdi et al. 2026 survey, 548 powerlifters: over 97.5% used varied practice (barbell variations plus accessories); 35–46% changed exercise selection as meets approached. **Low** (describes practice, not efficacy) — [PubMed 41166696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41166696/)
- Pallarés: full squats increased full, parallel, and half squat 1RMs more than partial-squat training. **Moderate** — [PubMed 31092132](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31092132/)

### Inferences
- **Strength/powerlifting template:** main lifts are the competition lifts (squat, bench, deadlift; overhead press for general strength). Close variations (pause squat, pause bench, deficit/block deadlift, close-grip bench, tempo squat) are **secondary** lifts that target weak points. Pause squat and sumo deadlift are legitimate main/secondary lifts **only** for this goal (sumo if it is the user's competition stance). Hypertrophy accessories then follow the hypertrophy tiers above.
- **Hypertrophy template:** the big three are useful but optional. Swap in high-SFR alternatives (hack squat/leg press for squat; RDL for deadlift).
- Encode a **goal-specific tier**: e.g., pause squat = C for hypertrophy, A for powerlifting; conventional deadlift = B/C for hypertrophy, S for powerlifting; overhead press = S for general strength, A for hypertrophy (upper body, covered by another researcher).

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis on competition lifts vs variations for powerlifting performance was found.
- The bench press and overhead press evidence is not covered here (upper-body scope).

## Q10. Summary "quality tier" table and attribute schema for the algorithm

### Takeaway
Combining the findings above: the generator should prefer stable, deep-ROM, easily progressed exercises that load the target muscle at long lengths (seated curl, hack squat/leg press, reclined leg extension, RDL, deep calf raises, cable crunch) and should demote high-fatigue, low-specificity, or hard-to-progress options (sumo/conventional deadlift and pause squat for hypertrophy, cable hip abduction/adduction, supermans, planks, farmer carries).

### Cited Findings
- The long-length principle is supported for hamstrings (Maeo 2021), quads (Pedrosa 2022; Larsen 2025), calves (Kassiano 2023), and glutes/adductors via depth (Kubo 2019). **Moderate overall** — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [PubMed 39699974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39699974/); [PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y)
- Lengthened partials beat full ROM in four of five studies, with the fifth showing no major difference; SBS calls the strategy promising but uncertain. **Low–Moderate** — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=57527)
- Wolf's review could not separate the muscle-length effect from the total-work effect. **Low** (secondary report) — [Elitefts](https://elitefts.com/blogs/training/long-muscle-length-training-for-the-big-three-what-the-2026-research-actually-means-for-your-squat-bench-and-deadlift)

### Inferences
**Suggested attribute schema (per exercise; integer 1–5 unless noted):**
- `long_length_loading` — how hard the exercise is near the target muscle's lengthened position
- `stability` — external stability (machine 5, barbell bilateral 3–4, unilateral free weight 2–3, cable unilateral 2)
- `resistance_curve` — enum: `lengthened_biased` / `flat` / `shortened_biased`
- `systemic_fatigue` — see Q8 values
- `overload_ease` — fine load increments and a stable rep range (machine/barbell 5; bodyweight-only like Nordic, plank, superman 1)
- `skill_demand`
- `joint_stress` (plus which joint)
- `muscles` — primary/secondary with credit fractions (e.g., squat: quads 1.0, glutes 1.0, adductors 0.75, hamstrings 0; rectus femoris credit lower than vasti)
- `goal_tier` — a separate S/A/B/C for `hypertrophy`, `strength`, `powerlifting`, `general_fitness`

**Main slot rules for hypertrophy (inferred):** the first lower-body compound should be from {hack squat, back/front squat to depth, leg press, pendulum squat}; the hinge slot from {RDL, SLDL, 45° back extension}; the hamstring curl slot from {seated curl > lying curl}. Add a reclined leg extension for the rectus femoris and a deep-stretch standing calf raise. Abs: cable crunch or weighted crunch.

**"Avoid as primary" for hypertrophy:** sumo deadlift, conventional deadlift (B at most, max once a week), pause squat, box squat, half/quarter squat, good morning as main hinge, cable hip abduction/adduction, supermans, planks, farmer carries, flat-floor calf raises, Nordic curl as the only hamstring exercise (hard to progress).

### Gaps
- Tier assignments are synthesized judgment. No study ranks exercises across all these attributes.
- Practitioner sources (Nippard, Israetel, Henselmans) were not directly retrieved; their rankings are broadly consistent with the inferences above, but that was not verified in this session.
