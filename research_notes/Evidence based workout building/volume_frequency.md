# Resistance-Training Volume and Frequency Dosing per Muscle, and How Session Size Should Scale with Time

Scope note: the key 2024 SportRxiv preprint by Pelland et al. has since been peer-reviewed and published as Pelland, Remmert, Robinson, Hinson, Zourdos, *Sports Medicine* 56:481–505 (2026), DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w. I read its full text (a PDF hosted by a third party; the publisher page was paywalled), and the numbers below come from that published version. The companion per-session paper (Remmert et al., "Is There Too Much of a Good Thing?") was still a SportRxiv preprint (v. 2025) in every source I could reach. Its full text was blocked (HTTP 403), so I took its numbers from the authors' university press release and from secondary write-ups.

Confidence labels used below:
- **Strong:** several meta-analyses agree.
- **Moderate:** one large meta-regression, or meta-analyses that conflict a little.
- **Weak:** a single study, a preprint, or a secondary report.
- **Practitioner:** coaching heuristic without trial data behind it.

## 1. Weekly sets per muscle: dose-response for hypertrophy and strength

### Takeaway
For hypertrophy, more weekly sets keep producing more growth, but each extra set buys less. A square-root curve fits best, with no clear plateau up to about 30–40 fractional sets, though the data above about 25 sets are thin. For strength, the curve flattens hard after about 4–5 fractional weekly sets on the lift being tested. Beyond that, frequency and specificity matter more than volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland 2026, scope:** 67 studies and 2,058 participants (mean age 25.2, ~79% male). Mean intervention 10.4 ± 4.5 weeks. Models were adjusted for intervention duration and training status. — [Pelland et al. 2026, Sports Med (PDF)](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf); [GainsFromGeebs summary](https://gainsfromgeebs.com/science/studies/resistance-training-dose-response-meta-regression-2026)
- **Pelland 2026, hypertrophy curve.** The volume model drew on 35 studies, 220 effects and 1,032 participants. The best-fit model was a square root. The posterior probability of a positive slope was 100%. The marginal slope was +0.24% muscle size per extra weekly fractional set (95% CrI 0.15–0.33), measured at the sample's mean of 12.25 sets/week. The authors describe diminishing returns that grow steeper with volume, "but not an inverted-U". — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate–Strong)
- **Pelland 2026, Table 3: hypertrophy efficiency tiers.** Each tier is defined by how many extra fractional weekly sets it takes to add one more detectable increment of growth (smallest detectable effect = 2.05% muscle size).

  | Tier | Fractional sets/week | Extra sets needed for one more detectable increment |
  |---|---|---|
  | Minimum effective dose | 4 | — |
  | Higher efficiency | 5–10 | ~6 |
  | Intermediate | 11–18 | ~8.5 |
  | Lower | 19–29 | ~10.75 |
  | Lowest | 30–42 | ~12.5 |
  | Unclear | 43+ | Not enough data; could even mean less growth |

  The authors add that "few studies have explored ~25+ fractional weekly sets." — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate)
- **Pelland 2026, strength curve.** The volume model drew on 66 studies, 490 effects and 2,020 participants. The best fit was a reciprocal model with "strong diminishing returns and a functional plateau". The marginal slope was +0.21% per set (95% CrI 0.16–0.26).
- **Pelland 2026, Table 4: strength tiers** (smallest detectable effect = 3.96%):

  | Tier | Fractional weekly sets on the tested lift | Note |
  |---|---|---|
  | Minimum effective dose | 1 | — |
  | Higher efficiency | 2 | ~0.75 extra sets per detectable increment |
  | Intermediate | 3–4 | ~2.25 extra sets per detectable increment |
  | Lower | 5+ | Extra sets "do not consistently enhance strength gains" beyond the smallest detectable effect |

  The authors caution that sets past 4 may still add strength gains smaller than 3.96%. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate)
- **Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (as summarised in Pelland 2026).** Hypertrophy rose 0.38% per extra weekly set. Groups doing at least 9 sets/muscle/week had ES 0.46 (95% CI 0.21–0.71), versus ES 0.32 (0.19–0.46) for under 9 sets. The difference was not significant (p = 0.076), and there were too few data to judge above 9 sets. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf); [Semantic Scholar record for Schoenfeld 2017](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Dose-response-relationship-between-weekly-training-Schoenfeld-Ogborn/0d34206f962394983054451cddd8a3b91818f732) (Strong for "more is better up to ~10")
- **Baz-Valle et al. 2022, J Hum Kinet 81:199–210.** This review compared 12–20 against more than 20 weekly sets. It found no difference for quadriceps (p = 0.19) or biceps (p = 0.59), and a result favouring more than 20 sets for triceps. The authors call 12–20 sets/muscle/week "an optimum standard recommendation" for young trained men. — [Baz-Valle 2022 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877); triceps detail via [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate; based on only 6–7 studies)
- **Ralston et al. 2017.** More than 5 weekly sets produced greater strength gains than 5 or fewer (SMD 0.18, 95% CI 0.06–0.30). The mean volume across those studies was low (3.14 ± 2.63 direct sets). — cited in [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate)
- **Pelland 2026 on reading the cut-points.** Triangulating Schoenfeld and Baz-Valle has led people to claim "substantial diminishing returns beyond ~12–20 sets". Pelland et al. argue this comes from categorical binning, and that the continuous model shows no hard cut-off. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- **ACSM 2026 Position Stand** (overview of 137 systematic reviews, more than 30,000 participants). Press summaries say it suggests about 10 sets per muscle group per week for hypertrophy, and 2–3 sets per exercise at ≥80% 1RM for strength. — [Medical News Today on the ACSM 2026 update](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/amp/articles/new-resistance-training-guidelines-debunk-myths-stronger-muscles-strength-size); [ACSM page](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) (I could not fetch the full text: HTTP 403)

### Inferences
- Treat 4 fractional sets/week as the floor for any trained muscle, and about 10 as the point where hypertrophy volume stops being "cheap". Past roughly 20, each detectable increment costs more than 10 extra sets. A generator can encode this as a utility curve: utility ∝ sqrt(fractional weekly sets).
- For strength goals, weekly volume on the competition lift itself can be modest (about 4–10 fractional sets). Extra sets help mainly through frequency (practice) and through hypertrophy of the muscles involved.

### Gaps
- The full numeric output from Remmert/Nuckols-style reanalyses was not retrievable.
- Pelland's training-status moderator results are on OSF and I did not inspect them. The paper says the moderators "not often influenced" the slope, and that they should be treated as hypothesis-generating.

## 2. Per-session volume ceiling ("junk volume")

### Takeaway
The best evidence available (a preprint) puts the point past which extra sets give no detectable additional hypertrophy at about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session. For strength it is about 2 direct sets of the lift per session. Gains beyond these points are small and uncertain, not negative. The generator's current cap of 6–8 sets per muscle per session sits below the evidence-based ceiling.

### Cited Findings
- **Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos 2025** (SportRxiv preprint, not peer-reviewed in the sources I found):
  - The probability of a positive per-session slope was 100% for both outcomes.
  - The "point of undetectable outcome superiority" (PUOS) came out at about 11 fractional sets per session for hypertrophy and about 2 direct sets for strength.
  - The authors note there is "insufficient data with very high per-session set volumes".
  
  — [SportRxiv preprint 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537); [EurekAlert/FAU press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1086804) (Weak–Moderate: preprint; numbers checked only through the press release and abstract snippets)
- **The authors' own advice:** for strength, a low number of heavy sets per session (1–2 sets, often >80% 1RM) spread across more frequent sessions. For hypertrophy, moderate per-session volume, with returns flattening past about 11 fractional sets. Senior author Zourdos says beyond this there is "a tipping point where the benefit of doing more becomes very questionable." — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1086804)
- **A secondary write-up** gives about 67 studies for the per-session analysis. It also claims that "another research group" found more than 16 sets/muscle/session hurt growth, but I could not identify or verify that claim's primary source. — [Biolayne Reps issue 36](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-36/too-much-muscle-math-finding-the-ideal-training-volume-per-session/) (Weak)
- **Pelland 2026 caveat:** one possible reason frequency showed no clear hypertrophy benefit is that "there is no plateau in hypertrophy with increasing per session volumes". — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- **RP Strength (practitioner):** gives weekly landmarks (about 6 sets/week maintenance, an example MRV of about 20/week) but no per-session caps. — [RP Strength, Volume Landmarks](https://rpstrength.com/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth/) (Practitioner)

### Inferences
- **Hypertrophy-oriented goals:** set the soft cap per muscle per session at 10–12 fractional sets. Allow up to ~12 for advanced lifters, and use ~6–8 for beginners because their weekly targets are lower anyway. A hard cap around 14–16 fractional sets reflects the data-poor zone (Weak).
- **Strength-oriented goals:** prescribe 2–5 heavy sets of the main lift per session. Push any further volume into variations and accessories, counted against the muscles' hypertrophy budget.
- **Why the current cap is a problem:** a 6–8 cap applied to *fractional* volume is especially restrictive. Chest pressing also adds 0.5 per set to triceps and front delts, so triceps can "fill" before any triceps isolation is added.

### Gaps
- I could not see the full per-session paper: model shapes, the interaction with weekly volume, and how results split by training status.
- There is no RCT evidence specifically on more than 12 sets/muscle/session in trained lifters that I could verify.

## 3. Fractional counting of indirect sets

### Takeaway
Count direct sets as 1.0 and synergist (indirect) sets as 0.5. This was the best-supported counting method, and it predicted both hypertrophy and strength better than counting indirect sets fully or ignoring them.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland 2026 definitions.** A direct set is one where the measured muscle is the primary force generator. An indirect set is one where the muscle is "meaningfully trained but not the primary force generator (i.e., synergist)".
- **Example:** 5 sets of curls plus 5 sets of rows gives biceps 10 total, 7.5 fractional, or 5 direct sets.
- **For strength:** direct sets are the exact tested exercise, and indirect sets are other exercises training the same muscles. Example: 10 sets of squat plus 5 of leg press = 12.5 fractional squat sets.

— [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)

- **Which counting method won:** all Bayes factors favoured the fractional method over total and direct-only counting. The authors conclude that the stimulus from an indirect set is, on average, "close to half" that of a direct set. They also say the fixed 0.5 should be read as a heuristic, "rather than a definitive standard." — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate)
- **The same counting applies to frequency**, so the curl-plus-row example above is a fractional frequency of 1.5. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- **RP (practitioner) counts differently:** only sets where the muscle is the prime mover, or the target of an isolation exercise, count. In other words, RP uses 0 for indirect sets. — [RP Strength](https://rpstrength.com/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth/) (Practitioner)

### Inferences
- **Weights to encode:** 1.0 for the primary mover and 0.5 for a meaningful synergist (for example triceps and front delts in pressing, biceps and rear delts in rows/pulldowns, glutes in squats, hamstrings in hip hinges). Use 0 for stabilisers only.
- **What the current generator likely does:** if it counts synergists at 1.0 (total counting), it overstates volume by about 25% in typical splits. That would make per-muscle caps trigger early. ([Root Strength worked example](https://www.rootstrengthseattle.com/blog/training-volume-dose-response): chest week 14.0 total vs 10.5 fractional; practitioner illustration.)

### Gaps
- No evidence supports graded weights such as 0.25 or 0.75 for specific exercises.

## 4. Frequency per muscle per week

### Takeaway
For hypertrophy, frequency barely matters once weekly volume is equal. Its practical role is to make room for the weekly volume within the per-session ceiling. For strength, frequency has a real positive effect with diminishing returns: going from 1 to 2 sessions per week on a lift is the big jump.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland 2026, frequency and hypertrophy** (volume-adjusted; reciprocal model): +0.32% per extra session per week, 95% CrI −0.14 to 0.82, with a 91.3% probability of being greater than 0. The authors call this "compatible with negligible effects". The direct-effects-only analyses showed no effect at all. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate–Strong)
- **This matches Schoenfeld et al. 2019:** no significant effect of frequency in volume-equated studies using direct hypertrophy measures (ES 0.07, 95% CI −0.08 to 0.21). — cited in [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Strong)
- **Pelland 2026, frequency and strength** (66 studies, 490 effects; reciprocal model): +3.27% per session (95% CrI 2.74–3.84), with a 100% probability of being positive. The control-adjusted estimate rose from 12.72% at a fractional frequency of 1/week to 17.32% at 2/week, and "beyond this point, accelerating diminishing returns occur." The authors attribute this partly to practising the lift. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Moderate)
- **An earlier meta-regression** (Grgic et al.) found no significant frequency–1RM relationship in volume-equated studies (p = 0.421). Pelland et al. say their non-linear models and added data refine that finding rather than contradict it. — cited in [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- **ACSM 2026 co-author Stuart Phillips:** training all major muscle groups at least twice a week matters far more than a "perfect" program. — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/amp/articles/new-resistance-training-guidelines-debunk-myths-stronger-muscles-strength-size) (Strong, as a consensus statement)
- **Iversen et al. 2021:** weekly volume matters more than frequency. — [Iversen 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34125411/) (Moderate)

### Inferences
- **Frequency rule:** muscle frequency per week ≥ ceil(weekly fractional target ÷ per-session cap of ~10–11), with a minimum of 2× per week for every major muscle.
- **What this means per split** (assuming 6 days/week PPL, 4 days/week upper/lower, 3 days/week full body):

  | Split | Hits per muscle per week | Supports up to (fractional sets/muscle/week) |
  |---|---|---|
  | PPL, 6 days | 2 | ~20–22 |
  | PPL, 3 days | 1 | ~10–11 (under-doses intermediate/advanced lifters unless sessions run near the cap) |
  | Upper/lower, 4 days | 2 | ~20–22 |
  | Full body, 3 days | 3 | ~30, but at ~6–8 sets/muscle/session for many muscles at once, which limits each session |

- **Strength and powerlifting:** train each competition lift (or a close variation) 2–3× per week.

### Gaps
- No study I found reports the frequency effect separately for trained and untrained lifters. The OSF moderator files were not inspected.

## 5. Differences by training level and goal

### Takeaway
The data barely separate the levels: the volume slope did not clearly change with training status. Level-based targets are therefore mostly practitioner convention. The gap between goals is clearer. Hypertrophy keeps rewarding volume, while strength rewards heavy specific practice and frequency and stops responding to volume early.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland 2026:** all models were adjusted for training status. Moderator analyses found the volume–hypertrophy slope "not often influenced by other variables", and the authors advise "extreme caution" with these indirect moderator results. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf) (Weak for level differences)
- **RP (practitioner):** for beginners, MEV sits close to maintenance volume (~6 sets/week). In advanced lifters MEV rises while maintenance stays put. Heavy compound lifts have lower MEV and MRV. — [RP Strength](https://rpstrength.com/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth/) (Practitioner)
- **Baz-Valle's 12–20 recommendation** applies specifically to young trained men. — [Baz-Valle 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877)
- **ACSM 2026:**
  - Strength calls for heavier loads (≥80% 1RM, 2–3 sets per exercise).
  - Power calls for 30–70% 1RM lifted as fast as possible.
  - Training to failure, equipment type and complex periodization did not consistently affect outcomes in average healthy adults.
  
  — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/amp/articles/new-resistance-training-guidelines-debunk-myths-stronger-muscles-strength-size) (Strong, consensus)
- **Iversen 2021, for time-limited general populations:** at least 4 weekly sets per muscle group in the 6–15RM range, with bilateral, multi-joint, full-range-of-motion exercises. — [Iversen 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8449772) (Moderate)
- **Root Strength (practitioner) tiers**, in fractional sets/muscle/week:

  | Level | Weekly sets | Frequency |
  |---|---|---|
  | New lifter | 6–10 | 2× |
  | 1–2 years | 10–16 | 2–3× |
  | Experienced, specialising | 16+ on priority muscles | 3× |
  | Concurrent sport athlete | 8–12 | 2× |

  — [Root Strength](https://www.rootstrengthseattle.com/blog/training-volume-dose-response) (Practitioner)

### Inferences
- **Get lean / weight loss:** I found no direct dose data in a calorie deficit within this scope. The defensible default is to keep moderate volume (~8–14 per muscle) so muscle is retained. Maintenance volume (~6/week, RP) is the floor. Don't cut volume to save fatigue unless recovery fails (Practitioner).
- **General fitness:** the ACSM and Iversen floor applies, at 4–10 sets per muscle per week, 2× per week (Moderate).

### Gaps
- There is no trial-level evidence for separate weekly targets for "advanced" lifters (more than 3–5 years of training).
- Volume needs during an energy deficit were not covered by the sources I reached.

## 6. Exercises per session, per muscle, and scaling with session duration

### Takeaway
No study tests "30 vs 60 vs 90 vs 120 minutes" directly. Duration should be treated as a budget, not a target. The right total session volume is the sum, over the muscles trained that day, of each muscle's weekly target divided by that week's frequency, capped per muscle at about 10–12 fractional sets.

A 120-minute push day can legitimately hold about 25–35 working sets. That means 6–9 exercises across chest, front/side delts and triceps. Only 4 exercises in 37 minutes reflects an artificially low per-muscle cap, not an evidence-based limit. If the volume caps are hit before the time runs out, the session should end early or add muscles not otherwise trained that day (calves, abs, rear delts). It should not keep piling sets onto saturated muscles.

### Cited Findings
- **Iversen 2021:** supersets, drop sets and rest-pause cut session time by roughly half at similar volume. Exercise-specific warm-ups are enough. — [Iversen 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34125411/) (Moderate)
- **Superset whole-body session in trained lifters:** 23 minutes (66%) shorter, with 4.2% less volume and higher perceived exertion. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/5cbd8a74af4545d7ae2d1741e24cc7c9) (Weak; single study)
- **10-week superset RCT:** strength and body composition changes similar to traditional training. — [Iversen group 2024, PubMed 39072654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39072654/) (Weak–Moderate)
- **ACSM 2026:** 2–3 sets per exercise for strength. — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/amp/articles/new-resistance-training-guidelines-debunk-myths-stronger-muscles-strength-size)
- **Session size from the per-session evidence:** for hypertrophy, gains plateau past about 11 fractional sets per muscle. For strength, past about 2 direct heavy sets per lift. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1086804)
- **Pelland 2026 limitation:** the trials did not track indirect costs of high dosage (sustainability, injury, burnout). Only participants who tolerated the training were analysed, so data on high-volume tolerance are biased toward people who coped. — [Pelland 2026](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)

### Inferences (encodable rules; all Practitioner / derived unless noted)

**Time cost per working set.** Roughly working time plus the rest after the set:
- Hypertrophy isolation: ~2.0–2.5 min (60–90 s rest)
- Hypertrophy compound: ~3.0–3.5 min (2–3 min rest)
- Heavy strength or powerlifting sets: ~4–5 min (3–5 min rest)

On top of that:
- Exercise changeover and warm-up: ~3–5 min for the first compound, ~1–2 min for later exercises
- Session overhead (general warm-up): ~5–8 min

These figures are my derivation. I did not retrieve a rest-interval meta-analysis for them.

**Working-set capacity by session length** (hypertrophy, straight sets):

| Session length | Working sets | Exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 30 min | ~8–10 | 3–4 |
| 45 min | ~12–15 | 4–5 |
| 60 min | ~16–20 | 5–6 |
| 75 min | ~20–24 | 6–7 |
| 90 min | ~24–28 | 7–8 |
| 120 min | ~30–36 | 8–10 |

For strength or powerlifting sessions, plan about 30% fewer sets. Supersets or giant sets add about 30–50% capacity.

**Generator algorithm:**
1. Per muscle per session, target = min(weekly target ÷ sessions hitting that muscle per week, per-session cap). Use a cap of 10–12 for intermediate/advanced lifters on hypertrophy goals, 8 for beginners, and 2–5 heavy sets per competition lift for strength or powerlifting.
2. Fill target sets with exercises of 2–4 sets each, using 1.0/0.5 fractional accounting. Exercises per muscle per session: beginners 1–2; intermediate and advanced 2–3 for large muscles (chest, back, quads, hamstrings/glutes) and 1–2 for small muscles (delts, arms, calves).
3. If time remains after every muscle on the day is at target, one of three things happens:
   - (a) Add volume to priority muscles, up to the cap.
   - (b) Add accessory muscles that fit the day: rear and side delts on push or pull days, calves and abs on leg days.
   - (c) Return a shorter session and tell the user it is complete.

   Don't exceed about 12–14 fractional sets per muscle per session; past that the evidence is thin and fatigue costs are likely.
4. If time is short (30 minutes or less), keep the per-muscle minimum (≥2–3 direct sets) and cut the number of muscles or use supersets. Never drop below a weekly floor of 4 fractional sets per muscle (Pelland's minimum effective dose).

**Do longer sessions add value?** Up to the point where every muscle trained that day reaches ~10–12 fractional sets, yes: weekly volume rises and so does hypertrophy (Moderate). Past that point the evidence suggests no detectable benefit (Weak–Moderate, preprint).

### Gaps
- I found no direct experimental comparison of session lengths at equal weekly volume.
- I did not find quantitative data on how fatigue from the first exercises (order effects) reduces quality in later sets during 90–120-minute sessions.
- The rest-interval evidence used to set time per set was not retrieved in this pass.

## 7. Recommended defaults table (synthesis for the generator)

The weekly targets are in fractional sets per muscle per week. The per-session numbers are fractional sets per muscle per session. The default sits inside the range, used when nothing else is specified. Bases for each row:

- Hypertrophy weekly numbers are anchored to the Pelland 2026 efficiency tiers (4 = minimum effective dose; 5–10 higher efficiency; 11–18 intermediate; 19–29 lower) and to Baz-Valle's 12–20.
- Per-session caps come from the Remmert 2025 PUOS (~11 fractional).
- Strength numbers come from the Pelland strength tiers (plateau ~4–5 fractional per lift per week), the Remmert PUOS (~2 direct per session) and ACSM 2026 (2–3 sets at ≥80% 1RM).
- Level splits are practitioner convention (RP, Root Strength).

**Confidence of the cells:**
- The ranges are Moderate.
- The exact defaults are Practitioner.
- "Get lean", "weight loss" and "general fitness" rows are Practitioner, anchored on ACSM 2026 (about 10 sets per muscle per week, each muscle at least 2× per week) and Iversen (≥4).

**Global rules**
- Never go below 4 fractional sets per muscle per week (the minimum effective dose).
- Count synergists at 0.5.
- Set frequency ≥ ceil(weekly target ÷ per-session cap).
- When the remaining session time exceeds what the volume targets need, add new accessory muscles before raising any muscle past its per-session max.
- Prefer ending the session early over adding junk volume.

### Inferences
- **Moving to these defaults would fix the reported bug.** For an intermediate lifter on a 120-minute PPL push day:
  - Chest gets about 10–11 sets (3 exercises).
  - Side delts get 6–8 (2 exercises).
  - Front delts get mostly fractional volume from pressing (~5) plus 0–3 direct.
  - Triceps get about 4–6 direct plus ~5 fractional from pressing (2 exercises).

  That is about 25–30 working sets and 7–8 exercises, roughly 85–105 minutes at hypertrophy rest intervals.
- **On a 3-day full-body split**, each muscle's per-session max should drop to about weekly target ÷ 3 (≈5–7). Otherwise sessions grow too long for the number of muscles involved.

### Gaps
- None of these exact cell values comes from a trial. They are interpolations between the meta-regression tiers and practitioner conventions.
- Separate targets by sex or for older adults (over 70, who were excluded from Pelland) are not supported by the data I found.
