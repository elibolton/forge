# Evidence-Based Exercise Selection: Upper Body (chest, lats, upper back, delts, arms, forearms)

Scope note: 15 tool calls of research. Direct longitudinal (training-study) evidence exists for only a handful of upper-body exercise comparisons (triceps overhead vs neutral, biceps preacher vs incline, lateral raise cable vs dumbbell, incline vs flat press, single- vs multi-joint for arms, free weight vs machine). For lats, upper back, rear delts, chest fly vs press, and forearms I found NO direct head-to-head hypertrophy trials; rankings for those muscles are inferences from biomechanics + the general principles below, and are labeled as such. Confidence levels: High / Moderate / Low / Speculative.

## Q1. Lengthened-position (long muscle length) and stretch-mediated hypertrophy: what is robust, and for which upper-body muscles?

### Takeaway
Training that loads a muscle hard at long lengths tends to produce equal or greater growth than training at short lengths; the best upper-body evidence is the triceps (overhead > neutral arm position, Maeo 2023). For biceps and delts the evidence is mixed or null, and lengthened partials are roughly equal to (not clearly better than) full ROM in the upper body of trained lifters. Encode "loads the long position" as a positive attribute, not as a dominant one.

### Cited Findings
- Maeo et al. (Eur J Sport Sci 2023, online 2022): overhead cable elbow extension vs neutral-arm cable extension, 12 wk within-subject. Triceps long head volume +28.5% vs +19.6% (1.5x), lateral+medial heads +14.6% vs +10.5% (1.4x), whole triceps +19.9% vs +13.9%, despite lower absolute loads overhead. Confidence: Moderate (single, well-run MRI study) — [PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/); [BarBend summary](https://barbend.com/overhead-triceps-extensions-vs-pushdowns-muscle-growth-study)
- Caveats on Maeo: the mono-articular heads (which do not change length with shoulder position) also grew more overhead, suggesting something beyond long-head length may be involved; comparator was a neutral-arm cable extension, not a classic pushdown; an earlier study (Stasinaki et al. 2018) found no significant difference between positions. — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/overhead-triceps-extensions-vs-pushdowns-muscle-growth-study); [Medium critique (opinion)](https://medium.com/@dougbrignole/review-of-scientific-studies-with-questionable-conclusions-part-one-f6c1332a61dd)
- Goto et al. (2017, as described by Milo Wolf at Stronger By Science): barbell skullcrusher partials at 90–45° elbow flexion vs full ROM: estimated triceps CSA +48.7% vs +28.2% over 8 wk. Classification as "lengthened" is a judgment call. Confidence: Low (estimated CSA, small study) — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/)
- Wolf et al. 2023 meta-analysis (Int J Strength Cond): partial vs full ROM pooled SMD 0.12 (95% CI −0.02 to 0.26) trivially favoring full ROM; subgroup suggested a possible benefit of lengthened partials vs full ROM (SMD −0.28, CI −0.81 to 0.16), CI crosses zero. Confidence: Low for lengthened-partial superiority — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/99e86b317bec40f5abc2f0008358737e)
- Wolf et al. 2025 (PeerJ), within-participant, 8 wk, ~30 resistance-trained people, one arm lengthened partials vs other arm full ROM for elbow flexors and extensors: similar thickness gains, statistics moderately favoring no difference. Authors still recommend emphasizing the stretched position (full ROM or lengthened partials). Confidence: Moderate that lengthened partials ≈ full ROM for arms in trained lifters — [PMC11829627](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11829627/); [PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/18904)
- Kassiano et al. (2022, JSCR) calves: lengthened-partial calf raises vs full ROM: lateral gastrocnemius ~+14.9% vs +7.3%, medial ~+15.2% vs +6.7%. This is a lower-body finding; the strongest lengthened-partial evidence is in calves, NOT the upper body. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/); [hipelife summary](https://hipelife.com/blog/lengthened-partials)
- Pedrosa et al. 2023 (Sports): 19 untrained women, one arm curled in the initial (long-length) range 0–68° vs the other in the final range 68–135° for 8 wk; initial-range training produced greater DISTAL biceps hypertrophy. Confidence: Low–Moderate (small, untrained, regional only) — [PMC9960616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9960616)
- Milo Wolf (SBS) argues lengthened partials probably do NOT cause true "stretch-mediated hypertrophy" (stretch time far too short vs animal/stretching data); long-length training likely increases fascicle length; overall confidence in long-length superiority "moderate to low." — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/)
- A 2025/2026 systematic review (Sports Med Health Sci) frames longer-muscle-length training as superior to shorter-length training for hypertrophy (largely lower-body data). — [SMHS](https://journal.hep.com.cn/smhs/EN/1255452794816139817)
- A multi-site cluster-trial preprint on lengthened partials reports greater distal hypertrophy that "needs replication across muscle groups" (not peer reviewed; full text inaccessible, 403). — [SportRxiv 486](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/486/version/622)
- Lateral raises: Larsen et al. 2025 (Frontiers in Physiology), 24 trained lifters, within-subject 8 wk: cable lateral raise (more load at long length) vs dumbbell lateral raise (load peaks at short length): Bayesian analysis gave moderate-to-extreme support for NO difference in proximal or distal lateral delt thickness (~3–5% growth). A long-length resistance profile did not help here. Confidence: Moderate — [PMC12277279](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12277279/)

### Inferences
- Robust enough to encode: (a) overhead triceps extension variants should outrank pushdowns for triceps growth; (b) exercises that reach a deep stretch under load (full ROM) should outrank shortened partials or exercises with no load at long length (e.g., floor press cuts off the bottom/stretched portion of the press — a negative attribute for chest hypertrophy).
- Not robust: "lengthened partials > full ROM" for upper body; "cable lateral > dumbbell lateral"; any claim of large stretch-mediated effects for chest, lats, or delts (untested longitudinally).
- Suggested weight in a scoring algorithm: long-length loading = moderate bonus (e.g., +1 tier step at most), with the triceps the clearest case. Do not let it override stability/progression/fatigue considerations.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal studies found comparing long- vs short-length loading for chest (e.g., deep DB press/fly vs pec deck), lats (e.g., pulldown with full stretch vs partial), rear delts, or traps.
- Could not locate a "Kassiano 2023 calves-not-upper" paper; the Kassiano lengthened-partial calf paper appears to be 2022 (JSCR). A Kassiano 2023 systematic review is cited secondhand only ([evipedia](https://evipedia.ai/lengthened_partials_er)), unverified.
- Could not verify a "Pedrosa 2022 elbow extension" study; Pedrosa's long-length work found is on knee extension (quads) and elbow flexion (2023).
- Zabaleta-Korta: the 2023 J Hum Kinet paper "Regional Hypertrophy: The Effect of Exercises at Long and Short Muscle Lengths in Recreationally Trained Women" appears to be the relevant one (preacher curl produced distal arm growth); a "2021" paper by that author was not found. — [PubMed 37559762](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37559762/)

## Q2. EMG vs longitudinal evidence — which to trust?

### Takeaway
Trust longitudinal hypertrophy data (MRI/ultrasound over weeks) over acute EMG. Use EMG only as a weak tiebreaker when no training study exists, and label those rankings low-confidence.

### Cited Findings
- Vigotsky, Halperin, Trajano & Vieira (Sports Med 2022, 52:193–199): acutely measured surface EMG amplitude is "not a validated predictor of muscle hypertrophy"; all three premises behind using EMG to rank exercises are weak. Confidence: High (methodological consensus) — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40279-021-01619-2.pdf); [QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/read/35006527/longing-for-a-longitudinal-proxy-acutely-measured-surface-emg-amplitude-is-not-a-validated-predictor-of-muscle-hypertrophy)
- Plotkin et al. 2023: hip thrust had greater gluteal EMG than squat, but this did not translate into greater gluteal hypertrophy — a concrete EMG-vs-outcome mismatch. — [Frontiers Physiol 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)
- Kassiano et al. 2025: incline curl (higher long-length loading) grew proximal biceps more, preacher grew distal more — muscle-length/regional effects that EMG would not predict. — [PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/)
- Stronger By Science (Wolf) calls EMG a poor predictor of growth. — [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/)

### Inferences
- Algorithm data model should store an `evidence_level` per exercise rating: `longitudinal` > `biomechanical_model` > `emg` > `practitioner_consensus`.

### Gaps
- None major.

## Q3. Free weights vs machines for hypertrophy (Haugen 2023)

### Takeaway
Hypertrophy is equivalent between free weights and machines; strength is modality-specific. So machines/cables are not a downgrade for hypertrophy and often win on stability, progression ease, and stimulus-to-fatigue; free weights carry extra value only when the goal includes free-weight strength (e.g., bench 1RM).

### Cited Findings
- Haugen et al. 2023 (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil): 13 studies, 1,016 participants, ≥6 wk. Hypertrophy: no meaningful difference (SMD −0.055, 95% CI −0.397 to 0.287; 5 studies). Free-weight strength tests improved more with free-weight training; machine tests tended to favor machine training (n.s.). Authors: choice depends on preference and goals. Confidence: Moderate–High for hypertrophy equivalence (few hypertrophy studies) — [PMC10426227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)
- Strength is specific to the trained exercise in multiple arm studies (Mannarino 2021; Kassiano 2025). — [Examine](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/tracking-sets-should-multi-joint-and-single-joint-exercises-be-counted-equally); [PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/)

### Inferences
- Encode `stability` (machine/cable/supported = high) as a positive for hypertrophy ranking (lower skill demand, easier to push near failure safely, easier small-increment progression), and `strength_transfer` as a separate attribute used only when user goal = strength.
- Barbell bench press should be "S for strength, A for chest hypertrophy"; machine/DB presses equal for chest growth.

### Gaps
- Few hypertrophy studies in Haugen; no exercise-specific (e.g., machine chest press vs barbell bench) upper-body hypertrophy trials verified in this research.

## Q4. Regional hypertrophy (upper vs lower chest, triceps long head, lats vs upper back, lateral/rear delt variants)

### Takeaway
Regional growth is real and exercise-dependent: incline pressing preferentially grows the upper (clavicular) pec; overhead extensions grow the triceps long head most; preacher vs incline curls shift growth distal vs proximal. Delt variant differences (cable vs DB lateral) appear negligible. No longitudinal data separate lats vs upper back by exercise.

### Cited Findings
- Incline vs flat bench: HD-EMG shows sternocostal head most active on flat, clavicular head most active at 45° incline (acute only). An 8-wk study in untrained men found similar pressing strength across flat, incline, and combined groups, with the incline group showing greater thickness gain at an upper-pec site. Confidence: Moderate for "incline biases upper chest" — [PubMed 34644424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34644424/); [PubMed 36334406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334406/); [Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42978-022-00200-1)
- Triceps long head: overhead +28.5% vs +19.6% neutral (Maeo). Confidence: Moderate — [PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)
- Biceps: Kassiano et al. 2025 (Int J Sports Med), 63 young women, 8 wk: incline curl > preacher for proximal elbow-flexor thickness; preacher > incline for distal. Strength followed specificity. Confidence: Moderate — [PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/)
- Zabaleta-Korta et al. 2023: preacher curl (max strain at long length) grew the distal arm in recreationally trained women. — [PubMed 37559762](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37559762/)
- Preacher vs "Bayesian" (behind-the-body) cable curl: similar thickness increases at all sites, no regional difference. — [PMC11906226](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11906226/)
- Nunes et al. 2020 (IJERPH 17:5859) actually compared cable vs barbell PREACHER curls (different resistance profiles) in untrained adults over 10 wk — not preacher vs incline. — [Motricidade review](https://revistas.rcaap.pt/motricidade/article/download/41706/30555/211712)
- Lateral delt: cable ≈ dumbbell lateral raise for proximal and distal lateral delt (Larsen 2025). — [PMC12277279](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12277279/)

### Inferences
- A chest slot generator should include at least one incline press (15–45°) per week alongside a flat/decline-ish press or fly to cover both pec regions.
- Triceps: at least one overhead extension per week; pushdowns as secondary.
- Biceps: pair a long-length curl (incline/Bayesian) with a preacher-type curl across the week for full-muscle coverage (Moderate confidence).
- Lats vs upper back (practitioner/biomechanical, Low confidence): vertical pulls and rows with elbows tucked/arm moving in sagittal plane bias lats (shoulder extension/adduction); wide-elbow rows and face-pull-type movements bias mid-traps/rhomboids/rear delts (scapular retraction + horizontal abduction).

### Gaps
- No longitudinal lat-vs-upper-back regional study by exercise found; a search for pulldown-vs-row hypertrophy returned only EMG/blog content.
- Rear delt variants (reverse pec deck vs cable reverse fly vs face pull vs rows): no longitudinal data found.
- Could not verify "Chaves 2024" incline-bench study.

## Q5. Compound-only vs adding isolation (do presses and rows train arms enough?)

### Takeaway
Mixed evidence. In untrained/short trials, compounds alone grow arms about as well; Mannarino 2021 found curls beat rows for biceps growth (~2x). A practical algorithm rule: count a compound set as ~0.5 set for the synergist (triceps on presses, biceps on pulls) and include direct arm isolation for hypertrophy goals.

### Cited Findings
- Mannarino et al. 2021 (JSCR 35(10)): unilateral DB row vs unilateral biceps curl, 8 wk: elbow-flexor thickness +11.06% (curl) vs +5.16% (row), p=0.009; authors recommend emphasizing single-joint work for elbow-flexor hypertrophy. Biceps only. Confidence: Moderate — [Paulo Gentil PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Single-Joint%20Exercise%20Results%20in%20Higher%20Hypertrophy%20of%20Elbow%20Flexors%20Than%20Multijoint%20Exercise.pdf); [Examine](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/tracking-sets-should-multi-joint-and-single-joint-exercises-be-counted-equally)
- 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis (Strength Cond J): similar hypertrophy for single- and multi-joint exercises; 6 of 7 studies were biceps/triceps. Confidence: Moderate — [Examine summary](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/tracking-sets-should-multi-joint-and-single-joint-exercises-be-counted-equally); [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4NG2avKl)
- Barbalho & Coswig 2020 (Eur J Sport Sci 20:72–79): "Single joint exercises do not provide benefits in performance and anthropometric changes in recreational bodybuilders." Numbers not retrieved. — [Solent repository](https://pure.solent.ac.uk/en/publications/single-joint-exercises-do-not-provide-benefits-in-performance-and/)
- Gentil et al. 2015 and Barbalho 2018 (trained women) compared MJ vs MJ+SJ; generally reported no added benefit of SJ (results not retrieved in detail). — [PubMed 26244600](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26244600/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/99f57dd84b4f4fee97c1dcdd1355844c)
- Examine rule of thumb: count a press as ~half a triceps set and a pull as ~half a biceps set. — [Examine](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/tracking-sets-should-multi-joint-and-single-joint-exercises-be-counted-equally)

### Inferences
- Algorithm: fractional set counting (compound = 1.0 for prime mover, 0.5 for synergists). Hypertrophy templates should still schedule direct biceps, triceps, side-delt and rear-delt work, because (a) Mannarino shows a benefit for biceps, (b) side delts and long-head triceps are poorly loaded by bench/rows (long head is shortened in pressing at the shoulder), (c) isolation adds volume at low systemic fatigue.
- Forearms: grip-intensive pulls give some stimulus; direct wrist curls/extensions are optional, low priority (no data found).

### Gaps
- Full numbers for Barbalho 2020 not retrieved. No trial isolated triceps long head growth from pressing alone vs pressing + overhead extensions.

## Q6. Ranked exercises per muscle with tier labels and attributes (S/A/B/C) + "avoid as primary"

### Takeaway
Tiers below combine the longitudinal evidence above with biomechanical reasoning (stretch loading, stability, resistance curve, stimulus-to-fatigue, progression ease, skill, joint stress). Only the triceps, biceps, side-delt, and incline-chest items rest on direct training studies; everything else is inference/practitioner consensus (Low–Moderate confidence). Tier definitions: S = default primary choice; A = excellent primary/secondary; B = fine accessory or substitute; C = situational/avoid as primary for hypertrophy.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence anchors for specific tier placements: overhead triceps > pushdown ([PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)); incline curl and preacher curl both effective, regionally complementary ([PubMed 39809454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39809454/)); Bayesian cable curl ≈ preacher ([PMC11906226](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11906226/)); cable ≈ DB lateral raise ([PMC12277279](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12277279/)); incline press biases upper pec ([Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42978-022-00200-1)); machines ≈ free weights for growth ([PMC10426227](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426227/)); curls > rows for biceps ([Mannarino PDF](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Single-Joint%20Exercise%20Results%20in%20Higher%20Hypertrophy%20of%20Elbow%20Flexors%20Than%20Multijoint%20Exercise.pdf)).

### Inferences
Attribute legend for encoding (1–3 scale): STR = load at long length/stretch; STAB = external stability; SFR = stimulus-to-fatigue; PROG = ease of progressive overload; SKILL = skill demand (3 = high); JOINT = joint stress (3 = high); STRG = strength-test value.

**Chest (pec major; clavicular = upper)**
- S: Incline DB or machine press (15–30°) — STR 3, STAB 2–3, SFR 3, PROG 3, SKILL 1–2, JOINT 1–2. Upper-pec bias (Moderate).
- S: Barbell bench press (flat) — STR 2, STAB 1, SFR 2, PROG 3, SKILL 2, JOINT 2, STRG 3. Best strength value.
- A: Flat DB press; machine chest press (Hammer-type); Smith machine incline press; weighted dips (lower pec, JOINT 2–3).
- A: Cable fly / pec deck — STR 2–3 (cable with deep stretch), STAB 3, SFR 3, isolation; good secondary.
- B: Push-ups (progression awkward unless loaded/deficit), DB fly (resistance near zero at top, heavy at stretch; shoulder stress if heavy), decline press.
- C / avoid as primary: Floor press (cuts off stretched bottom ROM — STR 1; a lockout/triceps or shoulder-friendly substitute only), svend press, landmine press as main chest lift, banded/light pumps.

**Lats (latissimus dorsi)** — no direct longitudinal comparisons found; Low confidence
- S: Lat pulldown (neutral/shoulder-width or wide; full overhead stretch) — STR 3, STAB 3, PROG 3, SKILL 1.
- S: Pull-up / chin-up (weighted when able) — STR 3, STAB 2, PROG 2 (bodyweight limits beginners), STRG 2.
- A: Chest-supported or cable row with elbows tucked (shoulder extension); single-arm cable pulldown/row (big stretch); machine pullover / cable pullover (lat isolation, STR 3).
- B: Barbell bent-over row (high SKILL/low-back fatigue → SFR 1–2, but good strength value), DB single-arm row (fine; lat bias when pulled toward hip).
- C / avoid as primary: Straight-arm pushdown as the only lat work (weak at long length with low load), farmer carries (grip/traps/core conditioning — not a lat or "pull-day" hypertrophy exercise), inverted rows for trained lifters.

**Upper back (mid/lower traps, rhomboids) and upper traps** — Low confidence
- S: Chest-supported row (T-bar / machine / incline DB) with wide elbows — STAB 3, SFR 3, low spinal load.
- A: Seated cable row (wide grip), Seal row, Barbell row (strength value; SFR lower).
- A (upper traps): DB/barbell/machine shrug — simple, progressable.
- B: Face pull (rear delt + mid-trap; small load), Rack pull (high fatigue), Meadows row.
- C / avoid as primary: Farmer carry (trap stimulus incidental, high systemic/grip fatigue; categorize as conditioning/loaded carry, not a pull-day hypertrophy slot), upright row (shoulder impingement risk; JOINT 3), band pull-aparts as main work.

**Front delts (anterior deltoid)** — receives heavy volume from all pressing; Low–Moderate confidence
- S: Seated DB or machine overhead press — STAB 2–3, PROG 3.
- S: Standing barbell overhead press — STRG 3, SKILL 2.
- A: Incline press (already counts substantial front-delt volume); Smith overhead press; landmine press.
- B: Front raise (DB/cable) — usually redundant given pressing volume.
- C: Arnold press as primary (awkward to progress), behind-the-neck press (JOINT 3 for many).
- Algorithm note: credit incline/flat pressing toward front-delt volume (0.5–1.0) so front raises are rarely needed.

**Side delts (lateral deltoid)** — Moderate confidence for lateral raises
- S: Cable lateral raise; DB lateral raise (equivalent per Larsen 2025); machine lateral raise — STAB 2–3, SFR 3, SKILL 1, JOINT 1.
- A: Lean-away / cross-body cable Y-raise; overhead press (some side-delt contribution).
- B: Upright row wide grip (effective but JOINT 2–3), Lu raise.
- C / avoid as primary: Heavy cheated barbell upright rows, band lateral raises (weak at bottom) for trained users.

**Rear delts (posterior deltoid)** — Low confidence (no longitudinal variant data)
- S: Reverse pec deck; cable reverse fly (cross-body, single-arm for stretch) — STAB 3, SFR 3.
- A: Chest-supported rear-delt DB raise; face pull (externally rotated); wide-elbow chest-supported row.
- B: Bent-over DB reverse fly (low-back fatigue, easy to cheat).
- C: Band pull-aparts as primary.

**Biceps (elbow flexors)** — Moderate confidence
- S: Incline DB curl (long-length, proximal emphasis); Bayesian (behind-body) cable curl; preacher curl (DB/machine/EZ; distal emphasis).
- A: EZ-bar / barbell curl (PROG 3, STRG 2), cable curl, hammer curl (brachialis + brachioradialis; also forearm credit), chin-ups (compound, partial biceps credit).
- B: Concentration curl, spider curl (short-length emphasis), drag curl.
- C / avoid as primary: Reverse barbell curl (wrist strain, small loads; a forearm/brachioradialis accessory, not a biceps primary), rows as the only biceps stimulus (Mannarino).

**Triceps** — Moderate confidence for overhead > neutral
- S: Overhead cable or DB triceps extension (single-arm or rope); EZ/DB skullcrusher with arms angled back (long-head stretch).
- A: Cable pushdown (rope/bar; easy progression, low joint stress; complements overhead); close-grip bench press (STRG 3, compound); weighted dips; JM press.
- B: Machine triceps extension / dip machine, cable kickback (short-length loaded).
- C / avoid as primary: Bench dips (shoulder stress, poor progression), DB kickbacks with free weight (zero load at stretch), diamond push-ups for trained lifters.

**Forearms (wrist flexors/extensors, brachioradialis)** — Speculative/Low; no hypertrophy trials found
- A: Hammer curl, reverse curl EZ-bar or cable (brachioradialis), DB/barbell wrist curl and reverse wrist curl (full ROM, behind-back or on bench).
- B: Farmer carry / heavy holds (grip strength more than size), plate pinches.
- Algorithm note: forearms should be an optional accessory slot, not inserted into pull days by default; carries belong in a "conditioning/carry" category.

**General generator rules derived from evidence**
1. Pick primary lifts only from S/A tiers; C-tier exercises only as explicit user choice or injury substitutions (e.g., floor press when shoulder pain limits the bottom range).
2. Weight `STR` modestly (strongest for triceps; neutral for side delts per Larsen 2025).
3. Do not penalize machines/cables for hypertrophy (Haugen 2023); add `STRG` bonus only when goal includes strength.
4. Use fractional set credit for synergists and still program direct arm and delt isolation.
5. Cover regions: incline + flat chest; overhead + pushdown triceps; long-length + preacher curls; vertical + horizontal pulls.

### Gaps
- Lats, upper back, rear delts, front delts, forearms: no direct longitudinal exercise-comparison data found; tiers there are biomechanics/practitioner-consensus (e.g., Menno Henselmans, Jeff Nippard — practitioner sources not fetched in this pass). Treat as Low confidence.
- Chest fly vs press hypertrophy: a blog attributes a fly-added-to-bench finding to "Ogasawara 2013," but it could not be verified and may be misattributed — [BoxRox (unverified)](https://www.boxrox.com/?p=234130).
- Joint-stress and SFR values are expert judgment, not measured quantities; no study quantifies SFR per exercise.
